- **Association Analysis**: Linear regressions adjusting for age and sex were used to test associations between total outlier count and composite cognitive score, MoCA and visuo-perception (Hooper Visual Organisation Test). Exploratory analyses tested associations with disease-specific measures (MDS-UPDRS, MDS-UPDRS-III, UM-PDHQ, HADS). Group comparisons for total outlier count used linear regressions correcting for age and sex. Mann-Whitney U tests with FDR correction compared proportion of outliers at each region. Hamming distance quantified inter-individual dissimilarity. Results: In DLB, total outlier count associated with poorer composite cognitive score (β=-2.01, SE=0.79, t=-2.54, p=0.02) and MoCA (β=-0.55, SE=0.27, t=-2.04, p=0.05). In PD, total outlier count associated with visuoperception (β=-0.67, SE=0.19, t=-3.59, p<0.01).
- **Key Findings Brief**: Neuroanatomical normative modelling revealed greater and more heterogeneous atrophy in DLB compared to PD, and in PD low versus high visual performers, with total outlier count significantly associated with cognitive performance in both conditions.
- **Key Findings Detailed**: The study demonstrated that neuroanatomical normative modelling can capture heterogeneous atrophy patterns in Lewy body diseases. Mean total outlier count was significantly higher in DLB (8.7±11.3) versus PD (3.60±6.0) (β=-5.60, SE=1.74, p<0.01), and in PD low visual performers (5.4±8.7) versus high performers (2.3±3.5) (β=-4.73, SE=1.30, p<0.01), differences not detected by conventional GLM analysis. Inter-individual dissimilarity (Hamming distance) was significantly greater in PD low versus high visual performers (W=522.5, p<0.01) and DLB versus PD (W=5649, p<0.01). In DLB, higher total outlier count associated with poorer composite cognitive score (β=-2.01, SE=0.79, p=0.02) and MoCA (β=-0.55, SE=0.27, p=0.05). In PD, total outlier count associated with visuoperception (β=-0.67, SE=0.19, p<0.01) but not global cognition. No associations found with MDS-UPDRS, MDS-UPDRS-III, UM-PDHQ, or HADS. Regional analysis showed 125/169 regions in PD and 147/169 in DLB had at least one outlier, with maximum 13.9% (PD) and 24% (DLB) of participants showing outliers in any single region, highlighting heterogeneity. Results remained robust with alternative outlier threshold (z<-1.282).
- **Key Limitations**: 1) Outlier threshold of z<-1.96 may miss subthreshold neurodegeneration, though alternative threshold showed similar results; 2) Site differences between UCL (3T Siemens, prospective collection with comprehensive clinical data) and NACC (1.5T GE, limited clinical data from relational database), with NACC DLB participants having longer disease duration and higher total outlier counts, potentially due to selection bias, inclusion criteria differences, or scanner differences; 3) Relatively small DLB sample (n=61), which may have underpowered correlational analyses, though consistent with other imaging DLB studies; 4) DLB patients generally frailer and more challenging to assess comprehensively; 5) Three participants (1 PD, 2 DLB) had much higher total outlier counts (45-53) than others, though imaging QC showed no abnormalities and clinical assessments were consistent with diagnoses.
- **Application Notes**: Total outlier count is agnostic to regional location of atrophy, unlike conventional GLM requiring same-location atrophy across individuals, making it particularly suitable for heterogeneous conditions. Can be calculated from freely-available automated pipelines (FreeSurfer) for commonly acquired T1w-MRI scans. Normative modelling uses reference dataset ~1000-fold larger than typical W-score approaches, capturing greater population variability. May have clinical utility as prognostic neuroimaging measure of disease progression in Lewy body disorders. Results demonstrate advantages over W-score metrics and group-average analyses. Authors suggest potential for personalized rather than group-average measures, holding promise for prognosis or treatment-response predictions for individual patients.

## General Notes

**General Notes**: This study applied a pre-trained normative model from Rutherford et al. (2022) rather than training a new model. PD participants were stratified by visual performance (high n=64, low n=32) using biological motion and Cats-and-Dogs tasks, shown to predict dementia risk, rather than by MCI status due to heterogeneity and reversion of PD-MCI. Visual classification: low performers scored worse than group median on both tasks. Controls: UCL recruited from patient spouses and volunteer databases; NACC from ADRC 8361 site. Scanning parameters: UCL 3T Siemens Prisma (1×1×1mm voxel, TE=3.34ms, TR=2530ms, flip angle=7°, 9min acquisition); NACC 1.5T GE (parameters in NACC database). Total 169 brain regions analyzed (148 cortical + 21 subcortical). Sensitivity analyses conducted excluding three potential outliers with very high total outlier counts showed similar PD results (visuoperception β=-0.38, SE=0.15, p=0.01) and trending DLB results (composite cognitive β=-0.94, SE=0.51, p=0.078; MoCA β=-0.33, SE=0.16, p=0.053). Regional comparisons: No FDR-corrected significant differences between PD high vs low visual performers; 16 regions showed significantly greater outlier proportions in DLB vs PD (FDR p<0.05), including temporal, occipital, limbic regions. Conventional GLM found no differences between PD high/low visual performers; identified only 3 clusters (precentral, superior frontal) in DLB vs PD comparison.
