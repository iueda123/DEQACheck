### Cam-CAN dataset

All the subjects were normal controls and were obtained from the Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience (Cam-CAN, [www.cam-can.org](http:/www.cam-can.org) ). The Cam-CAN is a large-scale collaborative research project at the University of Cambridge, launched in October 2010. This project used epidemiological, cognitive, and neuroimaging data to understand how individuals can best retain cognitive abilities into old age. The fMRI scans of the Cam-CAN dataset were preprocessed using Automatic Analysis calling functions from Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM 12) [1]. A detailed description of the dataset and pre-processing pipeline can be found in reference [2. A more general overview of the Cam-CAN dataset can be found in reference [3]. Demographic information can be seen in Table S1 and MRI scanner with image-acquisition protocol information is as shown in Table S2.

**Medication treatment**

In the study, 8 patients were drug-naïve patients with first-episode psychosis, and 7 patients taking antipsychotics irregularly were willing to re-start antipsychotic treatment following a 2-week medication-free period. All other patients had a discontinued medication period before this hospitalization (range from 2 weeks to 11 months, Mean ± SD: 2.69 ± 2.73 m). In the 6 weeks treatment, two patients were treated with first-generation antipsychotic medications alone (haloperidol, *n* = 1; sulpiride, *n* = 1), 25 patients were treated with second-generation antipsychotic medications alone (olanzapine, *n* = 7; aripiprazole, *n* = 3; risperidone, *n* = 5; iloperidone, *n* = 4; blonanserin, *n* = 3; ziprasidone, *n* = 3; aripiprazole and risperidone, *n* = 3; aripiprazole and aripiprazole, n = 1 ), and 8 patients were treated with a second-generation medication and a first-generation medication (risperidone and haloperidol, *n* = 2; aripiprazole and haloperidol, *n* = 1; olanzapine and haloperidol, *n* = 1; clozapine and haloperidol, *n* = 2; quetiapine and haloperidol, *n* = 1). The doses of antipsychotic medications were converted to chlorpromazine (CPZ)-equivalent doses.
