- **Association Analysis**: Classification performance metrics (BACC, SPE, SEN, AUC) used to evaluate diagnostic accuracy (AD vs CN) and prognostic capability (pMCI vs sMCI). Comparison with normative modeling (using 2σ threshold), classical ML methods (SVM with RBF kernel, LASSO logistic regression), and deep learning methods (ROI-based CNN, subject-based CNN, patch-based CNN from Wen et al. 2020). Sensitivity analysis with swapped training/testing datasets.
- **Key Findings Brief**: HAVAs demonstrated very accurate AD detection (AUC ≥ 94% on external datasets AIBL and MIRIAD) and accurate discrimination between progressive and stable MCI (AUC = 78%), outperforming normative modeling, classical ML, and state-of-the-art deep learning methods.
- **Key Findings Detailed**: For diagnostic task (AD vs CN), HAVAs obtained 88% BACC and 94% AUC on AIBL (467 CN/82 AD), and 89% BACC and 96% AUC on MIRIAD (23 CN/46 AD). For prognosis task (pMCI vs sMCI), HAVAs achieved 73% BACC and 78% AUC on ADNI MCI cohort (255 sMCI/235 pMCI). HAVAs outperformed the second-best method by 3 percentage points for both diagnosis and prognosis. Individual structures performed as follows on AIBL: amygdala (80% BACC, 89% AUC), hippocampus (80% BACC, 88% AUC), inferior lateral ventricle (79% BACC, 89% AUC). Compared to state-of-the-art methods on AIBL AD vs CN: multimodel HAVAs (88% BACC) > ROI-based CNN (84%) > LASSO HAV (85%) > normative modeling (81%) > normative hippocampus (70%). Sensitivity analysis with swapped datasets (training on AIBL/OASIS/MIRIAD, testing on ADNI) yielded 87% BACC and 93% AUC, demonstrating robustness to training domain. The screening analysis of 33 brain areas showed that amygdala (AUC=88%), hippocampus (AUC=87%), and inferior lateral ventricle (AUC=82%) were the most discriminant structures on the training ADNI dataset. Models combining multiple structures (HAVAs) consistently outperformed single-structure models across all evaluation metrics and datasets.
- **Key Limitations**: The study does not explicitly model site/scanner effects despite using multi-site data, which could introduce systematic bias. The training dataset is heavily weighted toward older adults from ADNI, with younger CN subjects from different cohorts. The AD model assumes a smooth pathological trajectory by mixing AD patients (55-96 years) with young CN subjects (<55 years), which may not fully capture the complexity of AD progression. Sample sizes vary considerably across datasets (e.g., MIRIAD has only 23 CN subjects). The method requires accurate segmentation from AssemblyNet, making it dependent on preprocessing quality. The polynomial models (quadratic/cubic) may oversimplify complex lifespan trajectories. Validation on MCI prognosis uses subjects from ADNI (same source as training CN/AD), potentially reducing independence. The paper does not discuss potential confounders such as education, APOE status, or comorbidities.
- **Application Notes**: Authors emphasize HAVAs simplicity makes it fully understandable and well-suited for clinical practice or pharmaceutical trials. The method uses very few parameters (<10) compared to CNN models (>10 million parameters), enabling better generalization and robustness to domain shift. Software package including AssemblyNet and HAVAs will be made freely available as downloadable Docker (https://github.com/volBrain/AssemblyNetAD) and online pipeline on volBrain platform (http://www.volbrain.net/). The authors note that while hippocampus volume is considered a hallmark of AD, amygdala volume provided better performance across all strategies when using single structures. HAVAs combines advantages of both normative modeling (easy interpretation) and classification approaches (accurate detection with pathology-specific decision boundary).

## General Notes

**General Notes**: This paper presents an innovative approach combining normative modeling with pathological modeling, creating dual lifespan trajectories for both CN and AD populations. The key innovation is using distance to both models rather than just deviation from normal. The study performs comprehensive whole-brain screening across 132 structures, 5 lobes, 4 regional tissues, and 3 tissues, identifying 33 areas with significant CN-AD divergence. The method explicitly models pathological trajectories by mixing AD patients (55-96y) with young CN subjects (<55y), assuming neurodegeneration is a slow progressive process. Statistical rigor is demonstrated through F-statistics, T-statistics, and Bayesian Information Criterion for model selection. The full dataset list mentioned in training includes: C-MIND (N=236 CN), NDAR (N=382 CN), ABIDE (N=492 CN), ICBM (N=294 CN), IXI (N=549 CN), OASIS (N=298 CN, N=45 AD), ADNI (N=404 CN, N=332 AD). Validation datasets: AIBL (N=467 CN, N=82 AD), MIRIAD (N=23 CN, N=46 AD), ADNI MCI (N=255 sMCI, N=235 pMCI). The paper demonstrates that methods based on amygdala consistently provide higher accuracy than hippocampus-only models, challenging conventional focus on hippocampus as primary AD biomarker. The study uses same selection criteria as ClinicaDL (Wen et al., 2020) for defining clinical groups, enabling fair comparison with deep learning methods.
