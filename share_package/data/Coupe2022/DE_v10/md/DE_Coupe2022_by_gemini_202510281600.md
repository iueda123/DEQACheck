- **Association Analysis**: The proposed HAVAs (i.e., the S AD score) reflects the probability for the subject under study to be a patient with AD (or a pMCI subject). The classification performance of the proposed method was validated using several metrics: balanced accuracy (BACC), specificity (SPE), sensibility (SEN) and area under the curve (AUC) based on HAVAs.
- **Key Findings Brief**: The proposed HAVAs score, based on hippocampal, amygdalo, and ventricular volumes, provides accurate AD detection and MCI prognosis, outperforming single-structure models and other state-of-the-art methods.
- **Key Findings Detailed**: The study developed a novel score, HAVAs, by combining volumes of the hippocampus, amygdala, and inferior lateral ventricle. This score showed high accuracy in distinguishing AD patients from controls (AUC >= 94%) and discriminating progressive from stable MCI (AUC = 78%) on external datasets. HAVAs outperformed single-structure models, classical machine learning, and deep learning methods. The simplicity and interpretability of HAVAs make it suitable for clinical use.
- **Key Limitations**: The authors did not explicitly state limitations, but they mentioned that the combination of normative modeling with learned features will be investigated in future works, suggesting a potential area for improvement.
- **Application Notes**: A software package including AssemblyNet pipeline and HAVAs estimation will be made freely available as a downloadable Docker and on the volBrain platform.
