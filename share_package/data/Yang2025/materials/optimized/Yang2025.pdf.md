Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes is the most common pediatric epilepsy, characterized by an age-dependent onset that typically arises during childhood brain development and is followed by remission at puberty. However, the heterogeneity in children's brain development at the individual level complicates the challenge of personalized treatment. Our goal is to quantify individual deviations from the normative range of brain morphometric variation in children with Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes and to assess their associations with clinical manifestations and cognitive functions. We have developed sex-specific normative models on regional subcortical volume, cortical thickness, and surface area data from 457 healthy children sourced from two datasets. These models were then utilized to map the brain morphometric deviations of children with Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes ( n = 187) and sex- and age-matched healthy controls ( n = 108) from another dataset. In the Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes group, children exhibited a higher proportion of regions with infra-normal deviations in subcortical volumes, the number of regions with normative deviations correlated with disease duration, seizure frequency, and Raven's total score. Our findings suggest that a few extreme distributions of heterogeneous brain morphometric deviations are present in a minority of individuals, emphasizing the need to monitor brain abnormalities throughout the course of the disease.

Keywords : brain development; brain morphology; individual-level heterogeneity; normative modeling; self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes.

## Introduction

Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes (SeLECTS), formerly known as benign Rolandic epilepsy, is a prevalent focal epilepsy in childhood (Specchio et al. 2022). The term 'self-limited' acknowledges the age dependency of both the onset and remission of this epileptic subtype. The age of onset for SeLECTS ranges from 3 to 14 years, peaking at 7 years, with remission typically occurring after adolescence. Children with SeLECTS experience recurrent seizures, often at critical developmental stages, which can have long-lasting impacts on their brain development. However, the heterogeneity in children's brain development at the individual level poses a significant challenge for personalized treatment. Understanding this variability is crucial for tailoring effective therapeutic strategies tailored to each child's unique needs.

Normative modeling offers powerful tools for assessing and predicting brain measures in individuals who deviate from the patterns observed in a normative reference population (Marquand et al. 2016; Gaiser et al. 2024). Conditioned by age, it furnishes estimates of neuroimaging phenotypes, including brain morphometry derived from structural and diffusion magnetic resonance image (MRI) data. For instance, normative modeling has been employed to describe brain volumetric development in infants (Dimitrova et al. 2021), and it has further established a correlation between deviations from normative development in preterm infants and factors such as respiratory, nutrition, birth weight, and neurocognition. Normative models have also been harnessed to create brain charts spanning the human lifespan, which have consolidated the largest neuroimaging datasets, mapped typical and atypical brain development and aging, and unveiled patterns of neuroanatomical variation across neurological and psychiatric disorders (Bethlehem et al. 2022). When applied to clinical phenotypes, studies have mapped the biological heterogeneity underlying the mental disorder and neurodevelopmental disorders, detecting individualized region deviations that differ from those found in the case-control study (Marquand et al. 2016; Rutherford et al. 2022a; Shan et al. 2022). Previous research has suggested that normative deviations outperform raw brain measures in estimating clinical conditions (Parkes et al. 2021). The accumulation of large sample datasets enables more precise normative distributions to be estimated (Bethlehem et al. 2022). For example, the CentileBrain framework provides an approach to normative modeling of brain morphometric measures through systematic empirical benchmarking and the identification of parameters that optimize model performance (Ge et al. 2024). Collectively, by providing statistical inferences relative to the normative range, parsing individual-level heterogeneity in clinical cohorts can expand the biological implications.

Previous studies on brain morphometry have reported atypical neuroanatomy in children with SeLECTS. Children with SeLECTS exhibited increasing thalamocortical structural connectivity within the motor circuit with age (Kwon et al. 2022) and showed increased regional gyrification index in the right posterior cingulate gyrus and increased sulcal depth in the right anterior cingulate gyrus and medial prefrontal cortex (Yin et al. 2024). Stratification of MRI data by age has provided evidence for variations in cortical thickness and white matter in children with SeLECTS (Smith et al. 2021). Furthermore, another study found larger gray matter volumes in the corticostriatal circuitry at the onset of epilepsy (Kim et al. 2015). The shape alterations of the putamen and caudate were also confirmed, and their relation with cognition depends on the side of epilepsy focus (Shakeri et al. 2017). While these case-control studies have detected group effects on brain morphometric measures,they do not quantify the interindividual variability that differs from case-control findings, nor do they identify potential regions that altered in a subset of children with SeLECTS. The use of normative modeling in this study compensates for limitations in these case-control studies and consider the following two points. First, the onset of children with SeLECTS often occurs during their critical period of brain development, and vulnerability in this process may be inferred from deviations from normative distributions. Second, neuroanatomical deviations may aid in monitoring possible language and cognitive dysfunction.

It is unknown whether morphometric measures deviations from healthy variation are present in individuals with SeLECTS and whether they are associated with clinical manifestation or cognitive function. In the current study, we employed normative modeling to estimate individual-level brain morphometric measures in children with SeLECTS. We hypothesized that children with SeLECTS would exhibit varying degrees of heterogeneity compared to the normal population. To test these hypotheses, we utilized the CentileBrain framework to derive normative model parameters based on the developing Chinese Color Nest Project (depCCNP) dataset and subsequently estimated deviations in brain morphometric measures to characterize the differences between children with SeLECTS and the normative range. Finally, we examined whether regional deviations in brain morphometric measures from the normative range were associated with clinical manifestation or cognitive function.

## Materials and methods

## Participants

The current work encompassed two independent cohorts: a casecontrol cohort and a normal sample for model training. The casecontrol cohort comprised 187 children with SeLECTS, including 98 boys and 89 girls, aged 4 to 18 years, with a mean age of 9.09 ± 2.66 years (mean ± SD). Additionally, the study included 108 controls, consisting of 61 boys and 47 girls, aged 4 to 15 years, with a mean age of 9.42 ± 2.49 years (Table 1). Epilepsy diagnoses were confirmed by two experienced neurologists, adhering to the International League against Epilepsy (ILAE) classifications (Berg et al. 2010). All participants had negative presentations on conventional (structural) MRI. Cognitive psychological assessments, such as Raven's Standard Progressive Matrices and the Integrated Visual and Auditory Continuous Performance Test, were administered to 75 children with SeLECTS. Raven's Progressive Matrices is a nonverbal test typically used to measure analog perception, reasoning, and abstract abilities and is regarded as a nonverbal estimate of fluid intelligence. The Integrated Visual and Auditory

Continuous Performance Test is a screening tool designed to distinguish people with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder from individuals with and without behavioral problems. It could also be used to monitor the effectiveness of neurofeedback training or medication. The study protocols adhered to the Helsinki Declaration of 1975 and received approval from the medical ethics committee of Jinling Hospital, School of Medicine, Nanjing University. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

To ensure the generalizability of the normative models, we included additional normal data to train the models. The data for model training consists of two samples: the CKG sample and the PEK sample. Samples from the two different centers are both sourced from the developing Chinese Color Nest Project (devCCNP). The devCCNP resource is accessible within the Chinese Color Nest Project (CCNP)-Lifespan Brain-Mind Development Data Community (https://ccnp.scidb.cn) hosted by the Science Data Bank. The two samples are combined to train the normative models, and the potential impact of site on performance is addressed by treating site as a random factor and using ComBatGAM (Pomponio et al. 2020) for site harmonization. These normal data comprise two samples: the CKG sample and the PEK sample. The two samples were combined to train the normative models, and both originate. This project enrolled typically developing school-age Chinese children and adolescents, and this study comprised 241 boys and 216 girls, aged 3 to 18 years, with a mean age of 10.44 ± 3.11 years. All normal participants had no history of neurological disorder or psychiatric illness and no gross abnormalities on brain MRI images. This project was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. Written informed consent was obtained from one of the participants' legal guardians, and written assent was obtained from the participants.

## Imaging protocol

Structural images were acquired on a Siemens Trio Tim 3.0tesla MRI scanner at Jinling Hospital. We used foam padding to minimize head motion. All participants were required to keep their eyes closed and to keep their heads still. Highresolution T1-weighted MRI data were acquired in a sagittal orientation using a magnetization-prepared rapid gradientecho sequence (repetition time/echo time=2,300/2.98 ms, flip angle=9 ◦ , field of view=256 × 256 mm 2 , matrix size = 256 × 256, slice thickness=1 mm, no interslice gap, and 176 slices). We also used T1-weighted MRI data provided by the devCCNP. Full details for the devCCNP have been published (Fan et al. 2023).

## Data preprocessing

The following processing was applied to both the SeLECTS casecontrol cohort and the devCCNP normal datasets. Structural MRI processing pipelines were primarily built using FreeSurfer (Fischl et al. 2001) and FSL (Smith et al. 2004). T1-weight data processing included cortical segmentation and surface reconstruction analyses. Anatomical surfaces were generated from the individual T1w image in native space using the recon-all command in FreeSurfer. Estimation of individual morphometric measures included the total intracranial volume, regional subcortical volume ( n =14), regional cortical thickness ( n =68), and surface area ( n =68).

These data were used to construct the models, primarily based on the following considerations. A positive or negative z -score signifies that the predicted value of the corresponding morphometric measurement lies above or below the normative mean, respectively. In accordance with previous literature (ENIGMA Clinical High Risk for Psychosis Working Group et al. 2024), we defined regional nonnormal z -scores as infra-normal when they fell below -1.96 or supra-normal when they exceeded 1.96, corresponding to the 5th percentile and 95th percentile, respectively. For each morphometric measure, we determined the percentage of participants with infra-normal or supra-normal deviations for each region, calculated the percentage of participants exhibiting infra- or supra-normal deviations across varying numbers of regions, and compared these percentages between the SeLECTS and control groups. SeLECTS, we assessed the associations between the percentage of individuals with infra- and supra-normal deviations and functional gradient.

Subsequently, we computed the averaged normative deviation for each regional morphometric measure and derived averaged deviation scores specifically for subcortical volume, cortical thickness, and surface area. Additionally, we determined the percentage of participants who exhibited an averaged infra-normal deviation or supra-normal deviation, which reflected a general trend of deviations falling below or exceeding the reference range, respectively.

## Estimation of normative deviations and observed morphometry

To evaluate whether normative deviations outperform observed morphometric measures, we employed support vector classification (SVC) models to predict the diagnostic status of children with SeLECTS. All the data (deviations of the three types of observed measures) were used together as inputs to classify using the SVC model. The regional z -scores and observed morphometric data were independently input into separate SVC models, utilizing 5-fold cross-validation implemented with a linear kernel from the scikit-learn package (version 1.2.2) (Rutherford et al. 2022b) following this procedure (Rutherford et al. 2022b). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was averaged across all folds. Additionally, we separately used each of the three measurements as inputs to compare the model performance.

## Statistical analysis

Group comparisons of the percentage of participants were conducted using the 2-proportion z -test implemented in R studio, while group comparisons of normative z -scores and observed measures employed the two-sample t -test. The association between the number of regions with infraor supra-normal deviations and clinical manifestations was examined using the Spearman correlation; the association between the averaged deviations of each morphometric measure and clinical manifestations was examined using Pearson's correlation. All statistical tests were considered significant at a threshold of P &lt; 0.05, and false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to account for multiple comparisons.

## Averaged infra- and supra-normal deviations

The distributions of averaged z -scores among children with SeLECTS and healthy individuals showed a nearly complete overlap (Fig. S3). Only one child with SeLECTS exhibited an averaged supra-normal deviation in surface area, while the remaining participants displayed a normal distribution for other averaged brain morphometric measures.

## Associations of regional deviations with clinical manifestations and cognition

Within the SeLECTS group, we did not identify associations of regional morphometric deviations with clinical manifestation and cognition. There was a positive association observed between the averaged regional z -scores of surface area and seizure frequency ( R 2 =0.06, P =0.001) (Fig. S4). When analyzing the associations of the number of regions with abnormal deviations with clinical manifestation and cognition, we found that the number of regions with supra-normal deviations in subcortical volume was positively correlated with the duration of disease ( R 2 =0.06, P =0.05) and seizure frequency ( R 2 =0.11, P =0.01), and the number of regions with infra-normal deviations in cortical thickness was positively correlated with Raven's total score ( R 2 =0.07, P =0.05) (Fig. 4).

## Comparison between normative deviations and observed measures

We evaluated whether normative brain regional z -scores have an advantage over observed morphometric measures in predicting diagnostic status. Regardless of whether regional z -scores or observed morphometric measures as inputs, linear SVC models failed to distinguish controls from the children with SeLECTS. Specifically, the models achieved an AUC of 0.56 when using regional z -scores and an AUC of 0.48 when using observed morphometric measures (Fig. 5). When a single morphometric measurement, whether the deviation value or the observed value, was used as the input, the performance of the model was inferior to that when the three measurements were used as inputs together (Fig. S5). Subsequently, we did two-sample t -tests between the two groups based on normative z -scores and observed measures, and we observed distinct patterns of atypical brain morphometry. A negative t -value in the normative z -scores comparison indicated morenegative deviations in the SeLECTS group (Fig. S6A), whereas a negative t -value in the observed z -scores comparison suggested thinner cortical thickness (Fig. S6B).

In this study, we found that the distribution of brain morphometric normative deviations in children with SeLECTS is similar to that of healthy children, with only a few extreme distributions present in a minority of individuals. There were no significant group differences across brain regions in terms of the percentage of participants with abnormal deviations. In the latest classification by the International League Against Epilepsy, the term 'benign' is no longer recommended because it does not acknowledge the presence of comorbidities in some individuals (Specchio et al. 2022). Children with epilepsy may develop neurological, psychological, and physical complications (Nickels 2022). Relevant risk factors encompass seizure frequency, seizure type, age, duration, and the adverse effects of antiepileptic drugs. Prior to seizures, developmental, cognitive, and neurological tests are typically normal, yet attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder or cognitive impairment may be present, often linked to speech and executive function impairments (Savas et al. 2020). Behavioral and neurological impairments may remit with age during the disease, though the underlying neural mechanisms for this phenomenon remain poorly understood. While the disease is self-limited within control, there exists heterogeneity in brain development among children. Numerous studies have affirmed that childhood epilepsy exhibits heterogeneity across various aspects, including genetic factors (Kugler et al. 2008; Rudolf et al. 2009; Strug et al. 2009; Xiong and Zhou 2017), brain activity (Gupta et al. 2014), cortical maturation (Siripornpanich et al. 2019), and clinical manifestations such as seizure timing (Vankadari et al. 2023). Consequently, investigating the heterogeneity of brain development in children with SeLECTS is crucial for elucidating the physiological mechanism underlying this disease.

Exploring the heterogeneity of brain development is a key focus of research in numerous neurodevelopmental diseases.

Normative modeling represents a prevalent approach for assessing individual-level deviations from the normative range and further mapping the associations between brain function and behavior (Marquand et al. 2019). Several studies suggest the potential benefits of normative modeling of brain developmental heterogeneity, including subtype segmentation grounded in disease heterogeneity, which aids in detecting disease biomarkers and enhances classification accuracy (Duan and Chen 2022). Additionally, studying the disease population further elucidates the clinical features of multisubtype disease, encompassing pathological complexity, symptom diversity, and therapeutic variability (Li et al. 2021). In this study, the percentage of children with SeLECTS exhibited infra-normal normative deviations in three morphometric measures was numerically higher than that of healthy controls in most brain regions, yet this difference did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 2). Notably, when comparing the infra-normal deviation of subcortical volume between the two groups, irrespective of specific brain regions, the SeLECTS group exhibited significantly higher deviations than the control group. However, through the traditional case-control comparison, the subcortical volume showed an overall decrement, yet this was not statistically significant. This underscores the value of normative modeling in studying individual differences, particularly as it reveals that a potentially small number of patients have alterations in certain brain regions.

The lack of strong statistically significant differences between epilepsy cases and controls obtained in this study suggests that the changes may occur at the level of the cortical microcircuits. The Chugani group has conducted classic studies, in which they used positron emission tomography to systematically analyze the developmental characteristics of different cortical regions by tracking changes in the number of synapses (Chugani et al. 1989; Chugani and Phelps 1991; Chugani et al. 1992). The development of cerebral cortex functions is intricately associated with the behavioral, neurophysiological, and neuroanatomical maturation processes in infants. A recent transcriptome analysis

indicated changes in cortical synaptic uniqueness across the lifespan and suggested that synaptic dysfunction contributes to neurodevelopmental disorders such as epilepsy (Wang et al. 2023). Moreover, studies have shown that the processes of overproduction and elimination of dendritic spines on pyramidal neurons in the human prefrontal cortex extend beyond puberty before stabilizing (Rakic et al. 1994; Petanjek et al. 2011), and this is related to changes in dopaminergic innervation (Wahlstrom et al. 2010; Islam et al. 2021). The development of a specific neuronal subclass of cortical neurons during early childhood indicates that the diffuse changes in functional connectivity during that period mayberelatedtoepilepsy (Petanjek et al.2019).These genetic,histological, and connectivity studies emphasize changes in microcircuits during cortical development. Although no significant correlation was found between the normative deviations and clinical features or cognitive function, we found that the number of brain regions exhibiting normative deviations was associated with disease duration, seizure frequency, and Raven's total score in children with SeLECTS. Previous studies have reported increased volumes of the amygdala (Salman et al. 2022) and putamen (Smith et al. 2021) in children with SeLECTS, with the latter considered a neuromarker of abnormal brain development (Luo et al. 2019). Subcortical structural abnormalities also encompass hippocampal asymmetry (Lundberg et al. 1999; Boxerman et al. 2007), although this controversial finding may be influenced by the radiologist's perception. This study offers a new explanation for these controversial results from the perspective of individual heterogeneity, suggesting that the limited sample size of abnormal subjects diminishes the effect in group comparisons. Additionally, a noteworthy finding in this study is the association between the functional gradient and the percentage of children with SeLECTS who have non-normal deviations in cortical thickness and surface area. A hypothesis is that variation in neuroanatomical measures in children with SeLECTS may be embedded within the architecture of the brain's functional gradient organization observed in healthy individuals.

## Limitations

There are several limitations pertinent to this study. First, our study could benefit from the inclusion of a larger sample of children, which would result in more stable and generalizable normative models. Secondly, the morphometric measures were based on cross-sectional and did not capture longitudinal changes. Studies have shown that cross-sectional models and brain measures may underestimate the extent of age-related brain changes (Di Biase et al. 2023). Future normative model training and application should consider incorporating both longitudinal and cross-modal data. Thirdly, the model only considers sex and age, and demographic, developmental, and lifestyle factors were not included in the analysis.

## Conclusion

Our study identified that the regional morphometric variations of children with SeLECTS were nested within the normal variation. The normative modeling offers new opportunities to explore the correlation between individual heterogeneity and clinical manifestations, which may potentially enhance the predictive power of neuroimaging in the future.
