## Deviations From Normative Functioning Underlying Emotional Episodic Memory Revealed Cross-Scale Neurodiverse Alterations Linked to Affective Symptoms in Distinct Psychiatric Disorders

Yang Xiao, Mingzhu Li, Xiao Zhang, Yuyanan Zhang, Yuqi Ge, Zhe Lu, Mengying Ma, Yuqing Song, Hao-Yang Tan, Dai Zhang, Weihua Yue, and Hao Yan

## ABSTRACT

BACKGROUND: Affective symptoms are a prevalent psychopathological feature in various psychiatric disorders. However, the underlying neurobiological mechanisms are complex and not yet fully understood.

METHODS: We used normative modeling to establish a reference for functional activation of functional magnetic resonance imaging based on an emotional episodic memory task, which is frequently used to study affective symptoms in psychiatric disorders. This normative reference was derived from a large dataset of healthy individuals ( n = 409) and used to evaluate individualized functional alterations by calculating deviations from this reference in a clinical dataset, which included 164 healthy control participants and patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) ( n = 56), bipolar disorder (BD) ( n = 31), and schizophrenia (SZ) ( n = 73). The functional deviations were mapped to emotional networks (ENs) with speci /uniFB01 c emotional functions and used to predict affective symptoms in different mental disorders. The microscale cellular signatures underlying macroscale variations were identi /uniFB01 ed using imaging transcriptomic analysis and associated with affective symptoms.

RESULTS: We observed distinct patterns of cross-scale neural alterations linked to affective symptoms in 3 psychiatric disorders. Macroscale neural dysfunctions in distinct disorders were embedded into non-overlapping ENs and signi /uniFB01 cantly associated with affective symptoms. Oligodendrocytes may mediate the network-speci /uniFB01 c impairments and microglia for MDD, astrocytes for BD, and excitatory neurons for SZ as replicable cell-type correlates of affective symptoms.

CONCLUSIONS: These /uniFB01 ndings revealed cross-scale neural alterations underlying affective symptoms in psychiatric disorders, providing a basis for understanding their neuropathological patterns and guiding individualized treatment.

Affective symptoms, a basic dimension of psychopathology, are highly prevalent across multiple psychiatric conditions (1). In clinical settings, affective symptoms in psychiatric patients manifest as disturbances in emotional processing, the result of which is emotional extremes in perception (2), attention (3), and response (4), and display signi /uniFB01 cant variability in distinct forms of clinical phenotype (5,6). Clarifying the neurobiological underpinnings related to psychiatric symptoms is a major challenge for contemporary research in psychiatry and would help advance the intervention optimization of psychiatric disorders (7 -9). However, the neurobiological alterations underlying affective symptoms in psychiatry are complex and remain poorly understood.

Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies have been a major step forward in characterizing macroscale neural dysfunctions related to affective symptoms in psychiatric patients (5,10,11). The emotional episodic memory task (12 -14), a widely used paradigm, effectively captures functional activation associated with adaptive memory-enhancing effects induced by emotional stimuli (15 -17). Maladaptive alterations in this task have frequently been observed in major depressive disorder (MDD) (18,19), bipolar disorder (BD) (20,21), and schizophrenia (SZ) (22,23), suggesting the neural dysfunctions of affective perturbation. Disrupted emotional memory retrieval has been reported that may manifest in multiple processes of pathological emotion in psychiatric patients, involving disturbances in emotional attention (24), perceptions (25), and regulation (26). Importantly, these disruptions were thought to link to the vulnerability and development of speci /uniFB01 c clinical affective syndromes (27,28), underscoring the task ' s relevance in assessing affective symptoms.

Studies using emotional episodic memory tasks have identi/uniFB01 ed altered neurofunctions related to affective symptoms in the amygdala and medial temporal lobe (MTL) system across various mental disorders (29 -31), suggesting heterogeneous patterns of neural dysfunctions in distinct diagnoses (18,32 -34). However, inconsistent results have often been reported, both within one speci /uniFB01 c diagnosis and across disorders. In MDD, increased activity in the amygdala and orbitofrontal gyrus has often been associated with emotional bias (18,19), but another study has shown no abnormalities (33). Hypoactivation in the amygdala and occipital gyrus has been recognized in patients with BD compared with patients with MDD (20,35), although an earlier study reported increased activity (36). Individuals with SZ have shown both hyperactivation and hypoactivation in the prefrontal and parietal cortices (37 -39), accompanied by coactivation with the MTL areas (37,38,40). Furthermore, while a study has reported higher activation of the cingulate cortex in patients with BD relative to patients with SZ (34), others have reported the opposite (41).

Although these mixed /uniFB01 ndings could be partially due to differences in task design, one more major reason is the individual differences within and across clinical cohorts, which were often not captured by traditional case-control designs (42,43). To address this, an individualized approach that disentangles the intersubject heterogeneity of neural dysfunctions that underpin affective symptoms is therefore necessary. Normative modeling quanti /uniFB01 es each patient as an extreme deviation from normative expectations for MRI-derived phenotypes based on clinical or cognitive characteristics by incorporating neural alterations into a continuous spectrum derived from a large healthy population (44,45). This method shares similarities with other dimensional methods commonly used to predict clinical variables from neuroimaging data (46). By cutting across diagnostic classi /uniFB01 cations, this approach shifts the focus from group-level analyses to individual-level deviations, thereby mitigating confounds arising from intersubject differences to some extent.

Complex clinical phenotypes of affective symptoms are not only associated with macroscale functional abnormalities but are also tied to alterations at the microscale genetic and cellular levels (47 -49). Studies of postmortem brain samples and animal models have consistently pointed toward cellular processes in the central nervous system, including pathological alterations of neurons and neuroglial cells, as important factors in the development of affective symptoms (50 -52). However, due to limitations in spatial resolution and coarse diagnostic classi /uniFB01 cations, the relationship between microscopic vulnerabilities, macroscale dysfunctions, and clinical phenotypes remains poorly understood. The Allen Human Brain Atlas (AHBA) microarray dataset has been utilized to investigate transcriptomic patterns linked to neuroimaging /uniFB01 ndings (53,54), with substantial evidence indicating a connection between gene expression and macroscale dysfunctions (55,56). The brainwide gene expression atlas has made it possible to bridge the gap between cross-scale functional alterations (57) and, by combining with a normative modeling framework, could identify the personalized multiscale associations of neural vulnerability.

In the current study, we aimed to investigate cross-scale neural dysfunctions related to affective symptoms in patients with MDD, BD, and SZ (Figure 1). By utilizing 2 task-fMRI datasets and a normative modeling framework, robust and individualized functional deviations during emotional memory retrieval were /uniFB01 rst identi /uniFB01 ed. Next, we embedded deviation patterns into macroscale emotional networks (ENs) to explore the functional annotations of emotional pro /uniFB01 ling associated with neural variability in distinct disorders and assessed the associations with affective symptoms dimension. Finally, we conducted transcriptomic association analysis to plausibly determine the cellular abnormalities linked to affective symptoms by integrating macroscale dysfunctions with microscale alterations from the perspective of brainwide transcriptome and brain single-cell sequencing. The normative modeling approach enables the disentangling of the cross-scale heterogeneous patterns of neural dysfunctions underlying affective symptoms within a single diagnosis while simultaneously identifying between-diagnoses commonalities and differences. Building on evidence of common disruption in emotional memory and explicit distinction of affective symptoms across different disorders, we hypothesized that individualized deviations would converge in limited regions but diverge signi /uniFB01 -cantly across large-scale networks between diagnoses.

## METHODS AND MATERIALS

## Participants

The participants of this study were drawn from 2 datasets. The healthy dataset included 409 healthy control (HC) participants (HCtrain group) recruited from the Center for MRI Research, Peking University. The clinical dataset consisted of 324 individuals: 56 individuals with MDD, 31 individuals with BD, 73 individuals with SZ, and 164 HC participants (HCtest group). These participants were recruited from the Center for Neuroimaging, Peking University Sixth Hospital. The study was approved by the medical ethics committee of Peking University Sixth Hospital (see the Supplement for details of the ethics reviews), and all participants provided written informed consent before taking part in the study. All psychiatric patients were assessed and diagnosed using DSM-IV criteria by experienced psychiatric physicians. The severity of depression in patients with MDD was assessed using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD), while the clinical symptoms of BD were assessed using both the HAMD and the Young Mania Rating Scale. The symptoms of patients with SZ were evaluated using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS). The HC participants had no current or lifetime history of Axis I psychiatric disorders or neurological disorders. See the Supplement for additional criteria for participants inclusion and exclusion and quality-control details.

The paradigm consisted of encoding and retrieval phases, and each phase included neutral and aversive images selected from the International Affective Picture System (59,60). ## Image Acquisition and Preprocessing

Imaging data were acquired from participants on a 3T GE Discovery MR750 scanner in the Center for MRI Research, Peking University (healthy dataset) and the Center for Neuroimaging, Peking University Sixth Hospital (clinical dataset). The high-resolution structural T1-weighted MRI and fMRI blood oxygen level -dependent signal were acquired and preprocessed using SPM12 (http://www. /uniFB01 l.ion.ucl.ac.uk/spm) with a standardized protocol (61). The details of imaging acquisition and preprocessing can be found in the Supplement.

## Functional Activation Analysis

The analysis of functional activation under task was conducted using SPM12. For each participant, a general linear model was used to identify functions activated by the task of each brain region de /uniFB01 ned by the Desikan-Killiany (DK) atlas (62). The regressors were constructed from the rest, aversive, and neutral blocks, and we selected the ' aversive -neutral ' activation maps in the retrieval phase as the effect induced by emotion. More details are described in the Supplement.

## Statistical Analysis of Individual Extreme Deviations in Psychiatric Patients

To investigate brain regions with signi /uniFB01 cant extreme deviations, we further de /uniFB01 ned extreme deviations by thresholding the individualized deviation ( z value) derived from normative modeling using z of 6 2.6 (corresponding to p , .005 in a standard normal distribution) (Figure S1), as was performed in previous studies (42,63). A percentage map quantifying the proportion of individuals showing an extreme deviation was calculated for each group, separately for positive and negative extreme deviations. The proportion difference ( D percentage map) of individuals with extreme deviations was assessed between each diagnostic group with the HCtest group. A nonparametric group-based permutation test was conducted to identify signi /uniFB01 cant brain regions for each diagnostic group, and statistical signi /uniFB01 cance was set at .0167 (.05/3) with multiple comparisons correction based on the false discovery rate (FDR). The statistically signi /uniFB01 cant positive and negative extreme deviations were de /uniFB01 ned as supranormal and infranormal, respectively. To avoid reliance on a single threshold, the D percentage map of each group was validated based on a threshold-weighted calculation for de /uniFB01 ning extreme deviations, which integrated results across a range of thresholds (see the Supplement). Region-level deviations were embedded into large-scale ENs. A well-recognized framework of 4 ENs was revealed by a recent meta-analysis study (64), with distinct spatial distributions and functional pro /uniFB01 les ranging from emotional generation to regulation (EN 1: working memory and response inhibition; EN 2: cognitive appraisal and language processing of emotional stimuli; EN 3: emotion perception and generation; EN 4: emotional reactivity), and positioned on the DK atlas (Figure S2 and Table S1). The signi /uniFB01 cant networks were then evaluated for each diagnostic group using a groupbased permutation test at the regional level. The details of the method can be found in the Supplement and Figure S3 and in previous studies (63).

## Association With Clinical Affective Symptoms

Based on a multiple linear regression approach, we used individual deviation of brain regions with signi /uniFB01 cant extreme deviations to predict affective symptoms in distinct diagnostic categories. The Pearson correlation and root-mean-square error (RMSE) between the observed scores and predicted scores were used to assess the model performance. The statistical signi /uniFB01 cance of correlation coef /uniFB01 cients was estimated by applying a nonparametric permutation test, and the signi /uniFB01 cance level was set at p perm , .05. Furthermore, we used the virtual lesion method to determine the feature weight of model. More details are provided in the Supplement.

## Imaging Transcriptomic Analysis and Cell-Type Enrichment

The brainwide transcriptome was acquired from postmortem brain tissue samples of 6 neurotypical adult donors derived from the AHBA (http://human.brain-map.org) and preprocessed using the abagen toolbox (https://www.github.com/ netneurolab/abagen) following established protocols (65). The spatial correlations between the expression of each gene and neuroimaging deviations were calculated separately at the group and individual level. Two brain single-cell gene sequencing datasets were acquired from the Lake et al. (66) and Li et al. (67) cell-type gene markers. Seven cell types were adopted following a previous study (54), including astrocytes, endothelial cells, excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, microglia, oligodendrocytes, and oligodendrocyte precursors. The fast gene set enrichment analysis method was applied to perform cell-type enrichment analysis (68) and then obtained normalized enrichment scores (NESs) at the group and individual level, respectively. Pearson correlations were used to evaluate the relationship between individual NESs and affective symptoms in distinct disorders. More details are provided in the Supplement.

## DISCUSSION

The current study supports the hypothesis that macroscale neural dysfunctions in different mental disorders are embedded within non-overlapping ENs and linked to speci /uniFB01 c domains of affective symptoms. Microscale cell type -speci /uniFB01 c signatures were associated with these dysfunctions, highlighting cellular processes underlying affective symptomatology. This provides critical insights into the neural mechanisms of affective symptoms in psychiatric disorders.

Mapping clinical symptoms to neurobiological alterations is signi /uniFB01 cant for developing effective interventions (8). The emotional memory paradigm used here reliably activates the MTL system (58), providing a robust framework for exploring neural dysfunctions underlying affective symptoms. However, individual heterogeneity limits consistency across studies. Normative modeling helped disentangle this heterogeneity, revealing considerable inconsistency in the functional alterations under task, with only a low proportion of the patients sharing extreme deviations in any single brain region (Figure S7). Thus, when constructing statistical models based on cross-sectional data, concerns regarding individual heterogeneity of task-based fMRI data should not be overlooked.

Macroscale alterations were largely distinct but showed some overlap. Hyperactivation in the right amygdala, left insula, and right supramarginal gyrus was common across all disorders (Figure S10). The amygdala and insula have been widely emphasized as underlying emotional impairments in mental illness, particularly in maladaptive responses to negative stimuli (5,69 -71), interfering with memory processing. The supramarginal gyrus, part of the default mode network, is linked to self-focused rumination (72,73). Its abnormal activation may reinforce cognitive biases, thereby contributing to distorted emotional and cognitive processing (14). The distinct patterns of macroscale dysfunctions in different diagnostic groups were embedded into non-overlapping large-scale ENs, with a notable increase in the proportions of individuals

with extreme deviations compared with the regional level (Figure S14). This suggests that the diverse de /uniFB01 cits could be aggregated within lesioned ENs, and these networks may align with core de /uniFB01 cits speci /uniFB01 c to each disorder. Patients with MDD exhibited hyperactivations in a network related to emotion perception and generation, suggesting a neural correlate for negative schema that could alter perceptions and evaluations of stimuli (74,75). This weakening has been hypothesized to underlie recovery (76,77), while strategies for regulating emotion perception have been proposed to alleviate depressive symptoms (26). In patients with SZ, abnormal regulations of working memory and response inhibition may underlie emotional /uniFB02 attening and overuse of suppression as a strategy (78,79). Patients with BD exhibited dysfunctions spanning both emotional reactivity and cognition, reinforcing the idea that mood instability in BD arises from imbalances between these systems (80). This provides a neurobiological basis for statespeci /uniFB01 c psychotherapeutic interventions tailored to depressive and manic states of BD (81). These network disruptions also carry predictive weights for affective symptoms (Figure S13), offering critical insights for optimizing clinical treatment strategies for affective symptoms.

Considering that previous studies indicated that cellular alterations possibly induced macroscopic variations linked to affective symptoms (47 -49), our study also bridged cellular abnormalities with macroscale dysfunctions. A transdiagnostic convergence emerged in oligodendrocytes, indicating that macroscale alterations may stem from disrupted betweenregion integration or segregation. Using lesion network mapping (8,82), circuit-level deviations were calculated, as had been conducted in a previous study (63). Most signi /uniFB01 cant functional circuit was aligned with disorder-speci /uniFB01 c ENs (Figure S15 and Tables S8 -S10), suggesting that dysfunctional circuits drive affective symptoms. Identi /uniFB01 ed cellular transcriptomic pro /uniFB01 les were signi /uniFB01 cantly associated with affective symptoms, showing that cellular abnormalities may act as vulnerability factors shaping macroscale dysfunctions and clinical outcomes. Given the heterogeneity of affective symptoms in different psychiatric disorders, the cross-scale neural alterations may provide enlightening evidence of unique dysfunctional neural characteristics.

Several issues need to be considered. First, the clinical sample size was relatively small, especially for a transdiagnostic analysis, and a larger sample would help to better parse clinical heterogeneity and improve the overall robustness of the /uniFB01 ndings. Second, non-overlapping measures for affective symptoms were used across different disorders. A more comprehensive assessment would help better characterize the affective symptoms of different mental disorders. Additionally, data on depressive severity in patients with BD were partially missing, making it impossible to calculate the anxiety/somatization factor of the HAMD to represent affective symptoms in BD. Third, most patients with BD in this study were in the euthymic stage. Further study with more patients in different stages is imperative to assess the reproducibility of our /uniFB01 ndings. Fourth, medication has long been considered as an important confounding factor. Despite sensitivity analysis being conducted to assess the potential impact of medication on the results (Figure S7), the possibility of confounding effects from medication cannot be entirely ruled out. Future studies should include a larger sample of unmedicated participants to validate our /uniFB01 ndings. Fifth, gene expression data from the AHBA were obtained from 6 healthy donors but may not fully represent the variability in cortical gene expression among individuals. Although the transcriptome association analysis operates under the assumption that cortical gene expression patterns exhibit a high degree of consistency across individuals (57), genes with greater interindividual variability might not have been adequately captured, potentially limiting the statistical power of associations. This limitation should be carefully considered when interpreting the /uniFB01 ndings. Additionally, gene expression data from the left hemisphere may not fully represent macroscale dysfunctions of the entire brain. Finally, cell-type enrichment analyses were based on independent datasets for imaging and transcriptomic data, with their relationship being inferred indirectly through spatial variations, and future research incorporating within-subject designs will be essential to directly assess the link between genetic and neuroimaging data.

## Conclusions

To summarize, this study revealed cross-scale neural alterations underlying affective symptoms in 3 common psychiatric disorders. Our /uniFB01 ndings identi /uniFB01 ed macroscale heterogeneous patterns of functional alterations during emotional episodic memory processing, with microscale cellular abnormalities mediating these changes. These /uniFB01 ndings advance our understanding of the neurobiology underlying affective perturbations and offer insights for clinical translation.
