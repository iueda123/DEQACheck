## A B S T R A C T

Background: The heterogeneity of the clinical symptoms and presumptive neural pathologies has stunted progress toward  identifying  reproducible  biomarkers  and  limited  therapeutic  interventions'  effectiveness  for  the  first episode drug-naïve major depressive disorders (FEDN-MDD). This study combined the dynamic features of fMRI data and normative modeling to quantitative and individualized metrics for delineating the biological heterogeneity of FEDN-MDD.

Method: Two hundred seventy-four adults with FEDN-MDD and 832 healthy controls from International Big-Data Center for Depression Research were included. Subject-specific dynamic brain networks and network fluctuation characteristics  were  computed  for  each  subject  using  the  group  information-guided  independent  component analysis. Then, we mapped the heterogeneity of the dynamic features (network fluctuation characteristics and dynamic functional connectivity within brain networks) in the patients group via normative modeling.

Results: The  FEDN-MDD  whose  network  fluctuation  characteristics  deviate  from  the  normative  model  also showed significant differences within the default mode network, executive control network, and limbic network compared with healthy controls. Furthermore, the network fluctuation characteristics are significantly increased in patients with FEDN-MDD. About 4.74 % of the patients showed a deviation of dynamic functional connectivity, and only 3.35 % of the controls deviated from the normative model in above 100 connectivities. More patients than healthy controls showed extreme dynamic variabilities in above 100 connectivities.

Conclusions: This work evaluates the efficacy of an individualized approach based on normative modeling for understanding the heterogeneity of abnormal dynamic functional connectivity patterns in FEDN-MDD, and could be used as complementary to classical case-control comparisons.

According  to  the  Diagnostic  and  Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (5th edition) (DSM-5), MDD is characterized by clinically manifesting at least five symptoms out of nine, and these different symptom combinations lead to the high heterogeneity of clinical  presentations  among  patients.  Profiling  the  individual  differences in patients infected with the same disease would be helpful for the understanding of the underlying neuropathology and the development of precision medicine.

Although abundant literature exists with regard to functional brain network  abnormalities  in  MDD,  only  a  very  few  robust  results  are replicative.  The  divergent  results  are  mainly  due  to  the  clinical complexity  of  MDD  together  with  the  differences  in  medication  and study protocols. In most fMRI studies, functional connectivity is typically assumed to be stable during the entire scan. There is an increasing interest in examining the dynamic changes in functional connectivity (Zhang et al., 2021), which could lead to the identification of specific dynamic connectivity alterations and explain the inconsistency in previous functional connectivity. However, it is unclear to what extent the inconsistently are attributed to the heterogeneity of the disease, or the variety of methods and measurements. The case-control paradigm assumes that the whole MDD patients are affected by the disease to the same  extent  and  are  uniform,  homogeneous  entities.  However,  this assumption is problematic because MDD is mainly diagnosed based on self-reported heterogeneous symptom combinations, and different participants may have different symptom profiles and different etiological pathways, and resulting neurobiological changes may converge on the same symptoms (Alexander-Bloch et al., 2014; Marquand et al., 2019). Therefore, to understand the neurobiology of MDD, it is important to understand  the  range  of  associated  neurobiological  variation,  which may subsequently inform intervention at the level of the individual in the spirit of ' precision medicine ' . Over the years, many different theoretical models have been proposed to explain the heterogeneity of psychiatric disorders. A normative model can be understood as a statistical model that maps demographic or behavioral variables to a quantitative brain readout, and we can consider the abnormal characteristics of each patient  as  an  extreme  value  within  this  distribution.  The  normative model can be used to fractionate heterogeneous clinical groups and to find outliers within the heterogeneous cohort. The normative model has been used in schizophrenia, bipolar, and autism spectrum disorders to map the variation within the patient's cohort and address heterogeneity by fractionating cohorts (Baldwin et al., 2022; Bethlehem et al., 2020; Chien et al., 2022; Marquand et al., 2016; Wolfers et al., 2018; Zabihi et al., 2019). Previous studies also confirmed that deviations from the normative  model  were  frequent  in  psychiatric  disorders  but  highly heterogeneous. In the present study, we used the normative modeling approach  that  maps  interindividual  deviations  in  patients  with  firstepisode  drug  naïve  depression  (FEDN-MDD)  in  reference  to  the healthy  range  (see  Fig.  1,  an  overview  of  the  proposed  normative modeling approach showing the steps in the pipeline). Firstly, a slidingtime  window  method  was  used  to  compute  the  dynamic  functional connectivity  matrices  for  each  participant.  Secondly,  subject-specific inherent connectivity networks and corresponding time-varying weight  variability  were  computed  for  each  subject  using  a  group information-guided  independent component analysis technique (GIG-

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Participants

We employed data from International Big-Data Center for Depression Research,  and  the  data  were  collected  from  multiple  hospitals/institutions in China (http://yanlab.psych.ac.cn/IBCDR). Full details on the datasets and preprocessing pipeline have been described previously (Yan et al., 2019). According to the information provided by IBCDR, we selected  subjects  for  group  statistical  analyses  from  1276  MDDs  and 1104  healthy  controls  through  the  following  criteria:  1)  FEDN-MDD aged from 18 to 50 were included, resulting in 276 FEDN-MDDs; 2) healthy controls with age in a range of 18 to 50 were included, resulting in 847 controls; 3) subjects with excessive head motion (mean FD &gt; 0.5 mm or max FD &gt; 2 mm) were excluded, resulting in 274 FEDN-MDDs and 832 controls; 4) 125 healthy control individuals (71 females, as a healthy-ICA group) of 832 controls were included to create the group level  components using GIG-ICA; 5) Another 289 healthy control individuals (165 female, as a healthy-base group) of 832 controls were included to establish a normative model; 6) 274 FEDN-MDD individuals (174 males) and the rest 418 healthy control individuals (232 males, as a healthy-comparison group) recruited from the same study sites were included to conduct statistical analyses. We divided the healthy controls into  three  groups  to  avoid  reusing  data  in  different  steps  of  analysis which might lead to biased results. There were no significant differences between the depression and healthy comparison control cohorts in sex and head motions (see Table 1). However, we detect a slightly older age (31.17y vs 30.13y) and a lower educational level (11.85y vs 14.23y) in the patient group.

## 2.2. Individual-level dynamic functional connectivity patterns

For each subject, we computed whole-brain time-varying connectivity  matrices  based  on m (m = 90)  ROIs  from  the  automated anatomical  labeling  (AAL)  template  (Tzourio-Mazoyer  et  al.,  2002) using a sliding time window method (window length = 60s) (Allen et al., 2014; Du et al., 2017) as dynamic functional connectivity patterns. For different  sites  with  different  TR  and  time-series  lengths,  the  timevarying  dynamic  connectivity  patterns  represented  by  a  window-byconnectivity matrix included the same length of connectivity strengths ( m × ( m GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1) / 2) and different numbers of the windows.

Group  information  guided  ICA  (GIG-ICA)  was  adopted  to  the window-direction concatenated dynamic connectivity patterns to extract the subject-specific independent components (ICs) as inherent networks  with  the  corresponding  weight  coefficients  reflecting  the networks' variability (Du and Fan, 2013; Du et al., 2017). The number of ICs  was  empirically  determined  to  be  20.  We  chose  the  healthy-ICA group to compute the subject-specific ICs at a group level, and then these group-level ICs were used as guidance information to compute ICs of  the  remaining  participants  (FEDN-MDD,  healthy-comparison  individuals, and healthy-base controls) using GIG-ICA. Finally, each subject was characterized by 20 inherent networks and time-varying weight coefficients.

Then, the time-varying weight coefficients were used to calculate the dynamic  characteristic  of  the  networks.  First  difference  method  was applied to compute the change of the weight as Δ t ( t = 1,2, … , number of windows GLYPH&lt;0&gt; 1), and the average value of absolute Δ t  greater than two times the standard deviation as the measurement of network fluctuation characteristic .  Similarly, we computed the changes in the strengths of functional connectivity, detected a limit of two times the standard deviation, and finally calculated the average value of the supra changes as the FC-dynamic characteristic. These two dimensions were parallel and complementary.

We also explored network-static and FC-static characteristics using the  proposed  approach,  and  the  method  and  results  were  in  supplementary materials.

## 2.3. Normative modeling

A  quantile  polynomial  regression  model  was  used  to  determine percentile  curves  based  on  the  second-order  polynomial  regression (Koenker, 2005; Lv et al., 2020). Compared to conventional regression seeking the mean of the variable to be predicted, quantile regression aims to seek the median or any other percentiles. It enabled statistical analysis  to  describe  the  full  range  of  normal  variation. For  infra-normal  individuals  with  index  i, z score  depends  on  its actual Ci and the value in the lower 5th percentile for the same age and sex. For supra-normal, z score depends on its real Ci and the value in the upper 95th percentile for the same age and sex. To avoid bias of the model, we used the range between the 5th and 95th percentiles for a given age and sex as scale coefficients to normalize z scores.

To assess generalization, we applied 10-fold cross-validation to build an aggregation normative model (Zabihi et al., 2019). We repeatedly trained the polynomial regression model on 90 % of the 289 healthybase  participants  at  different  percentiles,  withholding  the  remaining 10  %  for  estimating  generalization  performance.  This  10-fold  crossvalidation  procedure  yielded  10  normative  models.  Each  of  these models generated a normative distribution based on quantile regressions with 95 % confidence intervals.

Having the aggregation normative model based on age and dynamic (network  fluctuation  characteristic  and  FC-dynamic  characteristic) characteristics,  we  next  positioned  each  FEDN-MDD  patient  and healthy-comparison participant within the normative range for healthybase adults of the same age and sex. Thus, each FEDN-MDD patient and the healthy-comparison participant was given 10 individual z scores. We adopted a certainty measure to evaluate the deviation reliability for each subject based on the 10-fold cross-validation normative model. Particularly, the certainty was measured by nz/n, where nz  is the number of nonzeroes  in  these  10 z scores,  and  n  is  the  total  number  of  crossvalidation models (here n = 10). A higher value indicated higher deviation reliability, and vice versa .  Subjects with higher certainty ( ≥ 0.8) were identified as infra- or supra-normal, and their robust z was the average of the nonzeroes z scores.

To further validate  the  method's  robustness  and  the  study's  main results, we replicated the experiment using the quantile linear regression model. The results were listed in the supplementary materials.

## 2.4. Statistical comparisons of network-dynamic characteristics between groups

Statistical comparisons of z scores yielding from network-dynamic characteristics  were  conducted  to  explore  the  differences  between groups. The pseudo two-sample t -test at a 5 % significance level (permutation tests, n = 10,000) was used to quantify the statistical significance of group differences.

## 4. Discussion

In this study, we mapped the biological heterogeneity of the patients with first-episode drug naïve depression, based on independent component  analysis  derived  brain  measures,  mainly  focused  on  the network-level and FC-level dynamic characteristics in different inherent networks. The individuals with more deviations came from the patient's cohort when the FC-dynamic characteristics were analyzed. The percentage of individuals whose network-dynamic characteristics deviated from the normative model showed a significant difference in DMN, left ECN, and limbic circuits. And the significant differences mainly come from the male patients.

Plenty  of  studies  have  revealed decreased  functional  connectivity  in  default  mode  networks  (Bluhm et al., 2010; Schultz et al., 2017), as others found the opposite results (Posner et al., 2016). A recent study reported that the FC within the DMN was also positively related to symptom severity(Yan et al., 2019), and another study revealed that the higher severity of depression was related to increased dynamic FC between the medial and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex within the default network (Kaiser et al., 2016). We found decreased functional connectivity between the prefrontal gyrus and occipital cortex and increased functional connectivity between the anterior  cingulate  and  lingual  gyrus  in  FEDN-MDD  patients,  even excluding the ' outliers ' .  However, in the present study, we failed to detect the relationships between abnormal functional connectivity with the severity of depressive symptoms. We believed this was confined by the limited measurements of symptoms. Published studies tried to use the identified neural signatures from case-control studies to classify the patients with MDD from control groups (Formisano et al., 2008; Gupta and Vig, 2019). However, some classification models either failed to distinguish patients from controls effectively or with limited representations,  evidenced  by  the  lower  generalization  accuracy  for  an  independent validation dataset. Our results suggested that the potential of biomarker  identification  was  likely  smaller  than  anticipated,  considering  the  vast  individual  differences,  almost  half  of  the  patients  are deviated on at least one brain network. This individual viability cannot collapse into one or two robust biological subtypes.

Dynamic functional connectivity to qualify the time-varying functional connectivity patterns across brain regions (Hutchison et al., 2013;

Ioannides, 2007) was getting more interest, since it provided unique information about communication among neural systems beyond that provided by static functional connectivity (Damaraju et al., 2014). In the present study, we used the sliding time windows correlation method (Allen et al., 2014; Tian et al., 2018; Yue et al., 2018), combined with the normative model, to analyze the distribution of the patients on the FCdynamic  characteristic  within  different  networks.  We  found  that compared to controls, more FEDN-MDD patients were identified as deviations, especially as supra-deviations. This result was consistent with previous  studies,  which  suggested  that  the  communications  between different brain areas were more unstable in MDD patients (Long et al., 2020). Kaiser et al. reported increased fluctuation in functional connectivity between MPFC and insula, and higher severity of depression related  to  increased  dynamic  functional  connectivity(Kaiser  et  al., 2016). Furthermore, researchers have also demonstrated that a combination of temporal and spectral characteristics of brain connectivity has the highest predictive accuracy in discriminating temporal lobe epilepsy patients from healthy controls (Chiang et al., 2018). These results suggested that the dynamic FC approach might represent more accurately the fluctuation nature of the brain. Indeed, we found that the distributions of the FEDN-MDD patients and controls were comparable when considering the functional connectivity as static and constant over the length of the scan.

The  combination  of  normative  model  with  functional  fluctuation might facilitate  the  future  study  in  identifying  the  more  represented dynamic biomarker for MDD. However, our findings should be considered in light of several limitations. First, the normative model describes the functional network trajectories along with age, which were based on cross-sectional data and should be validated in a longitudinal cohort.

X. Lin et al.

Second, we did not find associations of the severity of symptoms with the  percentage  of  deviation  from  the  normative  model,  and  the  deviations we identified may be biologically meaningful yet unrelated to psychopathology. Another potential reason is lacking of enough clinical information  and  cognition  function  evaluation.  Third,  the  choice  of window length may have a potential influence on the results.

## 5. Conclusions

In  conclusion, we estimated a normative model of dynamic functional connectivities in different brain inherent networks, based on a large MDD cohort. We found that the distributions of FEDN-MDD and controls showed more difference when considering the dynamic characteristics compared to static features in functional connectivities. Our results showed that it was necessary to look at the limitation of the casecontrol  paradigm -which collapses individual  patients  with  different neural signatures and even symptoms into one ' average MDD participant ' ,  and  further  understanding  the  heterogeneous  neuroimaging mechanism of MDD. Normative modeling might provide an excellent tool for understanding the heterogeneity of psychiatric disorders, and can be used as complementary to classical case-control comparisons, and this  study  also  posed  a  warning  for  the  limited  potential  of  the biomarkers in psychiatric disorders.
