Figure  S1.  Flowchart  of  Cerebellar  Segmentation  Process  for  Patients. The preprocessing  of  patients'  MRI  3D  T1  images  was  conducted  to  remove  the brainstem  region  and  prevent  erroneous  segmentation  of  the  enlarged  brainstem. First, the brainstem was manually delineated using the "Draw" tool in MRIcron, and the marked region was saved as a VOI file. The "Draw/Advanced/Subtraction Plots" function  was  then  used  to  subtract  the  marked  brainstem  region  from  the  original image, resulting in brainstem-excluded images. Next, the preprocessed images were imported  into  the  ACAPULCO  software  package,  where  the  cerebellum  was automatically  segmented,  including  region  localization  and  lobular  segmentation. Two  neurosurgery  specialists  independently  reviewed  the  segmented  images  to ensure  accuracy.  Any  segmentation  inaccuracies  were  manually  corrected,  and  the final results were determined through consensus. Cases that could not be corrected manually or involved significant discrepancies were excluded. Due to the variability in the segmentation boundaries of lobules VIIIA and VIIIB across participants, these two lobules were merged into a single lobule (VIII). Finally, the volumes of the 26 cerebellar lobules were calculated based on the corrected segmentation maps.

Figure  S2.  Scatter  Plot  Showing  the  Correlation  Between  tOC  and  Tumor Volume. A  significant  positive  Pearson  correlation  was  observed  between  tumor volume and tOC (total outlier count) in patients (r = 0.41, p &lt; 0.001), indicating that larger tumor volumes are associated with higher tOC values. The blue line represents the  linear  regression  trend,  while  the  shaded  area  denotes  the  95%  confidence interval.

## Supplementary table

Table S1 summarizes the p-values from multiple regression analyses evaluating the impact  of  various  clinical  variables  on  behavioral  outcomes.  The  variable  tOC demonstrates significant associations with several behavioral outcomes, particularly Withdrawn  Behaviors  (p  =  0.008),  Social  Problems  (p  =  0.046),  and  borderline significance for Attention Problems (p = 0.081), Delinquent Behavior (p = 0.091), Total Problems (p = 0.063), and Internalizing Problems (p = 0.068). These findings underscore the critical role of cerebellar atrophy in influencing withdrawal behaviors, social  problems,  and  internalizing/externalizing  problems.  Similarly,  DIPG  shows strong significance for several outcomes, including Thought Problems (p = 0.014), Attention  Problems  (p  =  0.015),  Aggressive  Behaviors  (p  =  0.003),  and  broader psychological measures like Total Problems (p = 0.010) and Externalizing Problems (p  =  0.006).  Other  variables,  such  as  Headache  and  Bucking,  are  significantly associated with specific outcomes, such as Social Ability (p = 0.019) and Somatic Complaints  (p  =  0.003)  for  headaches,  and  Social  Problems  (p  =  0.033)  and Attention  Problems  (p  =  0.035)  for  bucking.  On  the  other  hand,  variables  like Weakness, Ataxia, and Diplopia show limited statistical significance across outcomes. Overall, these results highlight the importance of cerebellar atrophy (tOC), DIPG, and specific symptoms like headaches in predicting behavioral and emotional challenges.
