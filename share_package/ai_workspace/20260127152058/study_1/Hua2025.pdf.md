## Techniques and Methods

Adjustment of Regional Cortical Thickness Measures for Global Cortical Thickness Obscures De /uniFB01 cits Across the Schizophrenia Spectrum: A Cautionary Note About Normative Modeling of Brain Imaging Data

Jessica P.Y. Hua, Susanna L. Fryer, Barbara Stuart, Rachel L. Loewy, Sophia Vinogradov, and Daniel H. Mathalon

## ABSTRACT

Recent neuroimaging studies and publicly disseminated analytic tools suggest that regional morphometric analyses covary for global thickness. We empirically demonstrated that this statistical approach severely underestimates regional thickness dysmorphology in psychiatric disorders. Study 1 included 90 healthy control participants, 51 participants at clinical high risk for psychosis, and 78 participants with early-illness schizophrenia. Study 2 included 56 healthy control participants, 83 participants with nonaffective psychosis, and 30 participants with affective psychosis. We examined global and regional thickness correlations, global thickness group differences, and regional thickness group differences with and without global thickness covariation. Global and regional thickness were strongly correlated across groups. Global thickness was lower in the schizophrenia spectrum groups than the other groups. Regional thickness de /uniFB01 cits in schizophrenia spectrum groups were attenuated or eliminated with global thickness covariation. Eliminating the variation that regional thickness shares with global thickness eliminated disease-related effects. This statistical approach results in erroneous conclusions that regional thickness is normal in disorders like schizophrenia or clinical high risk syndrome.

Whether to adjust for variance associated with global brain metrics is a perennial question in brain morphometry studies (1 -4). Recent studies in psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia and bipolar disorder (4 -6), as well as the clinical high risk for psychosis (CHR-P) syndrome (7), have advocated that regional morphometric analyses covary for global mean thickness to support conclusions about disease-related regional speci /uniFB01 city. Moreover, recent publicly disseminated analytic tools for conducting ageand gender-normative modeling incorporate global covariation when deriving regional measurements without an option to omit global thickness covariation (8). These analytic approaches have been applied to large datasets, yielding seemingly de /uniFB01 nitive estimates of disease effects on region of interest (ROI) morphometry (5 -7). Multiple studies in which the global thickness variance was removed showed little to no disease effect on regional thickness pro /uniFB01 les (5 -7). However, this global thickness adjustment approach has some /uniFB02 aws (9), and in the current article, we statistically demonstrate how this approach leads to erroneous conclusions about thickness in psychiatric disorders.

Historically, neuroimaging studies have adjusted for head size or intracranial volume (ICV), thereby removing nuisance variance because somatic size variation, particularly ICV variation, is not typically affected by normal aging (10) or disease processes that systematically affect regional volumes or thickness. Because the variance removed is largely irrelevant to aging and/or disease effects on the brain, ICV-adjusted ROIs are more sensitive to these effects (1). Additionally, age-related ICV changes are present in children undergoing somatic growth, including head size and brain growth, which partially accounts for the observation that covarying for ICV reduces erroneous nonlinear effects of age from childhood to young adulthood (11). Interestingly, recent studies have documented ICV changes associated with older age (12,13), challenging the assumption that ICV is /uniFB01 xed in adulthood, although the effects have been small and the underlying mechanisms not fully understood. Notwithstanding these recent studies, it remains evident that global thickness, like the ROI thicknesses from which it is derived, is affected by normal aging (14,15) and disease processes (4,6,16 -19) to a much greater degree than ICV. Therefore, it is impossible to remove global thickness variance from ROI thickness measures without removing agingand disease-associated variance, rendering global thickness -adjusted ROI measures less

sensitive to these effects. Because ICV is less related to regional thickness, age, and disease-related processes, adjustment of regional thickness measures for ICV does not have the same deleterious impact as adjustment for global thickness.

For example, if a disease such as schizophrenia (4,6,16 -19) affects cortical thickness in a correlated, widespread manner, controlling for global thickness will attenuate or eliminate disease effects on regional thickness. In effect, the question being tested is not about disease-related regional speci /uniFB01 city, but rather, ' What would effects on thickness in a given region be if this region ' s thickness was uncorrelated with global thickness or if we only analyzed the aspects of regional thickness that are uncorrelated with global thickness? ' Given that global and regional thickness measures are strongly correlated, it is not clear that the scienti /uniFB01 c question addressed when using globally adjusted ROI thickness metrics is conceptually meaningful. Indeed, ROI thickness measures are so distorted when deprived of their shared variance with global thickness that disease effects are attenuated or eliminated, as shown in studies of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder (4 -6).

To provide empirical support for the above arguments, we examined regional thickness pro /uniFB01 les from 2 archival magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) studies. Study 1 included healthy control participants (HCs), participants at CHR-P, and participants with early-illness schizophrenia (ESZ) who underwent MRI scanning at the University of California San Francisco. Study 2 included HCs, participants with nonaffective psychosis (NAff-P), and participants with affective psychosis (Aff-P) who underwent MRI scanning as part of the HCP-EP (Human Connectome Project for Early Psychosis). We hypothesized that 1) covarying for global thickness would attenuate or eliminate patient group regional thickness abnormalities; 2) this would be especially true when there are patient group abnormalities in global thickness and when global and regional thickness are highly correlated; and 3) adjustment for ICV would not have the same detrimental effect.

## METHODS AND MATERIALS

## Study 1 Participants

Participants at CHR-P who were antipsychotic naïve ( n = 51) met criteria for attenuated psychotic symptoms or brief intermittent psychotic states and/or were at genetic risk with deterioration in functioning criteria (Table S1). Ten individuals at CHR-P converted to psychosis during the course of the study, and 23 were followed clinically for $ 24 months without converting. Participants with ESZ ( n = 78) met DSM-IV criteria for schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder, with onset occurring within 5 years of study entry. HCs ( n = 90) did not meet DSM-IV Axis I criteria and did not have a /uniFB01 rst-degree relative with psychosis. See Hua et al. (18) and the Supplement for details.

## Study 2 Participants

Participants with NAff-P ( n = 83) met DSM-5 criteria for schizophrenia spectrum disorder (20), with onset occurring within 5 years of study entry (Table S1). Participants with Aff-P ( n = 30) met DSM-5 criteria for major depression with psychosis or bipolar disorder, with psychosis onset within 5 years of study entry. HCs ( n = 56) did not meet DSM-5 criteria for a mood or psychotic disorder (20) and did not have a /uniFB01 rstdegree relative with a schizophrenia spectrum disorder. See Hua et al. (17) and the Supplement for details.

## Procedures

Protocols were carried out in accordance with each study ' s respective institutional review board and the Declaration of Helsinki. Participants provided written informed consent, or parental written consent and participant assent was obtained for minors. Comprehensive regional MRI morphometric analyses have been published for studies 1 (18) and 2 (17). In this article, we focused on cortical thickness and the effect of global adjustment. Study 1 [https://osf.io/2h8uj/ (21)] and study 2 (NIMH Data Archive [https://nda.nih.gov/], doi: 10.15154/ kgg6-yf92/) data are available online.

## MRI Processing

For MRI acquisition and harmonization details, see the Supplement. Global mean cortical thickness and ROI thicknesses (22) were computed using FreeSurfer (ROIs are numbered in /uniFB01 gures and listed with assigned numbers in Table S2).

## z -Scoring Normalization

Without Global Thickness. ROI thicknesses were regressed on age, gender, and site (only study 2 included site) in HCs to model the effects of normal aging, gender, and site. Resulting parameter estimates were used to derive predicted ROI values for each participant, irrespective of group, which re /uniFB02 ected the expected thickness for a healthy individual of a speci /uniFB01 c age, gender, and site. Regional thickness values were transformed to adjusted z scores (23 -25) (see Supplemental Methods) by subtracting predicted values from observed values and dividing by the HC model standard error of regression. Adjusted z scores represent each participant ' s deviation from the value expected for an HC of the same age, gender, and site.

With Global Thickness. Consistent with previous studies (4 -7) and analytic tools (8) developed to derive ROI thickness z scores adjusted for normal age, gender, global thickness, and site (only study 2 included site), we regressed cortical ROIs on these variables in HCs. Parameter estimates were used to derive predicted ROI thickness values, which were subtracted from observed values and divided by the HC model standard error of regression. Adjusted z scores represent the participant ' s deviation from the value expected for an HC of the same age, gender, global thickness, and site. Adjusted z scores, with or without global thickness adjustment, were used in pro /uniFB01 le and permutation analyses.

## Analyses

Global Thickness Analyses. Analyses of covariance were used to examine raw global thickness group differences, covarying for age, gender, and site, and were followed with Bonferroni comparisons.

## Global and Regional Thickness Associations Analy-

ses. To test for group differences in the slopes of the relationship between ROI thickness and the global thickness raw score, each ROI was regressed on group, global thickness, and the group 3 global thickness interaction. Nonsigni /uniFB01 cant interactions were dropped, and the global thickness common slope across groups was estimated. Signi /uniFB01 cant interactions were followed up with estimation of ROI versus global thickness regression slope within group.

Regional Thickness Analyses. Multifactorial repeatedmeasures multivariate analyses of variance (ANOVAs) compared 2 aspects of pro /uniFB01 les: 1) nonparallelism (group 3 ROI interaction) and 2) level differences (group main effect). Nonparallelism was followed with 2-group group 3 ROI multifactorial repeated-measures multivariate ANOVAs. Nonparallelism of 2-group multifactorial repeated measures multivariate ANOVAs was followed with Bonferroni-corrected simple effects comparing groups on each ROI.

Permutation Analyses. To test the effect of adjusting ROI thickness for global thickness when the ROI was uncorrelated with global thickness, we arbitrarily selected the rostral middle frontal (RMF) gyrus, which showed a group difference and expected within-group correlation with global thickness. A 1way ANOVA assessed the group effect on RMF z scores without adjustment for global thickness. Next, a 1-way ANOVA was used to test the group effect on RMF z scores with global thickness adjustment. Then, global thickness raw scores were scrambled within group to arti /uniFB01 cially attenuate correlations with RMF thickness, and norm-adjusted z scores with global thickness adjustment were recalculated. Modi /uniFB01 ed RMF z scores were subjected to a 1-way ANOVA to assess the effect of group. Group differences were followed up with Bonferronicorrected 2-group comparisons.

Sensitivity Analyses. We examined the effect of accounting for a different global covariate, ICV. Using the same methods as above (but for ICV instead of global thickness), we examined ICV group differences, ICV and regional thickness associations, and regional thickness pro /uniFB01 les accounting for ICV for studies 1 and 2.

## DISCUSSION

It has been argued that ' failing to account for global [cortical thickness] changes could lead to erroneous conclusions about the speci /uniFB01 c involvement of particular regions in the development of the disorder ' (4). We have illustrated how statistically adjusting for global thickness, but not ICV, leads to attenuation or elimination of true disease effects on regional thickness, especially when psychiatric groups differ in global thickness and when global and regional thicknesses are highly correlated.

Schizophrenia spectrum samples in both studies had a thinner global cortex than other groups, consistent with previous research (4,6,16 -19). Global thickness was strongly correlated with regional thickness within all groups. However, regional widespread schizophrenia spectrum group differences (large effect sizes) were eliminated with global thickness adjustment, even though adjustments were modeled using only HC data. In fact, global thickness adjustment distorted schizophrenia spectrum group pro /uniFB01 les so much that, paradoxically, adjusted z scores for some ROIs were rendered abnormally thicker. In contrast, the CHR-P and Aff-P pro /uniFB01 les did not differ from the HC pro /uniFB01 le in global thickness, and the pro /uniFB01 les remained similar with global thickness adjustment. Furthermore, permutation analyses showed that distortion of true group differences only arose when global and regional thickness measures were highly correlated, which in nature is most of the time. Arti /uniFB01 cially attenuating the regional global thickness correlation resulted in global thickness -adjusted z scores that preserved true group differences evident when analyzing non -global thickness adjusted z scores. In contrast to global thickness, as expected, ICV did not show the same strong correlations with regional thicknesses (26). Moreover, groups did not differ on ICV, and regional thickness ICVadjusted pro /uniFB01 les closely resembled pro /uniFB01 les without global thickness adjustment.

Thus, legitimate concerns arise regarding studies that purport to examine regional speci /uniFB01 city of thickness abnormalities in schizophrenia and psychotic spectrum disorders using global thickness adjustment (4 -7). Three studies found that widespread thinning in schizophrenia was eliminated with global thickness covariation (4 -6). A fourth study of participants at CHR-P examined global thickness -normalized ROI z scores using a norm-adjustment toolbox (8) that provides no option to omit global thickness adjustment (7). Their conclusions (7) challenged the utility of regional thickness as a psychosis risk biomarker. These global thickness -adjusted study conclusions contrast starkly with conclusions from 2 largescale studies that found 1) accelerated thinning among CHRP converters versus nonconverters (27) and 2) widespread thinning in participants at CHR-P versus HCs, particularly in CHR-P converters, resembling previously observed schizophrenia de /uniFB01 cits (28). Furthermore, in study 1, although the small sample size for our CHR-P conversion analysis was a limitation, and there was no signi /uniFB01 cant level difference between converters and nonconverters, there was complete attenuation of any level difference with global thickness covariation, with the level difference effect size diminished after the effect of global thickness covariation was accounted for. Accordingly, previous global thickness -adjusted analyses may not provide an accurate account of thickness abnormalities in schizophrenia (4 -7).

These analytical methods are important to clarify because conclusions drawn from these studies inform our understanding of disease pathophysiology and the advancement of prognostic biomarkers. We argue that future studies that examine regional thickness in psychiatric disorders abandon statistical global thickness adjustment and that publicly disseminated tools include the option to omit global thickness adjustment (9).
