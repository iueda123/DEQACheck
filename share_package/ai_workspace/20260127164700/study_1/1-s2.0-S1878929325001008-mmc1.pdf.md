## Supplementary Methods

## Model Construction:

The  Generalized  Additive  Models  for  Location,  Scale,  and  Shape  (GAMLSS) framework was used to construct growth curves for each corpus callosum morphological  characteristic.  GAMLSS  allows  for  flexible  modeling  of  nonlinear growth patterns through smoothing techniques. Specifically, we experimented with the pb (P-splines) function provided by GAMLSS, which constructs a design matrix and applies  smoothing  to  capture  complex  trends  in  the  data.  However,  we  observed overfitting with this method and selected a second-order polynomial function of age based on prior research and experimentation with simpler models.

## Distribution Choice:

To identify the most suitable distribution for our data, we used built-in functions in the GAMLSS package to compare multiple candidate distributions, including Normal and Box-Cox t (BCT) distributions. Model selection was based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). AIC evaluates the model's fit quality, while BIC penalizes model complexity more stringently, especially in large sample sizes. The BCT distribution demonstrated the best performance based on BIC values and was selected as the optimal model for our analysis.

## Confidence Interval Estimation:

To  estimate  confidence  intervals  for  the  growth  curves,  we  employed  bootstrap resampling.  This  involved  generating  1000  resamples  from  the  original  dataset  and constructing growth curves for each resample. From these models, we calculated the 95%  confidence  interval  for  the  50th  percentile  (median)  of  the  growth  curves, providing robust estimates of growth trends.

Visualization of Age-Specific Means and Variability

To complement the growth curves, we generated error bar plots that display the mean and standard error for each morphological characteristic within each age group. These plots provide a more granular view of variability and allow for direct comparisons of means between sexes in specific age ranges, supplementing the trends observed in the growth curves. This approach supports a detailed understanding of the distribution and variability of corpus callosum development across ages.
