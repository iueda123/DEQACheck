## Supplemental Methods

Participants in Dataset I

Participants  were  recruited  by  the  Department  of  Psychiatry  in Affiliated  Brain Hospital  of  Nanjing  Medical  University  (NBH)  and  Nanjing  Drum Tower  Hospital (NDTH) between September 2011 and December 2020. According to the DSM (4 th Edition), all depression patients were diagnosed by at least two attending doctors and assessed  by  the  Hamilton  Rating  Scale  for  Depression  17-item  (HAMD-17)  for depression severity. HCs were recruited from these two local communities, respectively. The  recruitment  and  exclusion  criteria  for  patients  and  HCs  were  detailed  in Supplemental  Methods.  Participants  with  incomplete  clinical  data  or  poor  imaging quality were also excluded. Ultimately, 127 depressed patients and 110 HCs in NBH were enrolled in this study as the discovery cohort. In addition, 60 patients and 56 HCs in  NDTH  were  used  as  an  external  test  set  to  evaluate  the  performance  and generalization of our classification model.

The  recruitment  criteria  for  patients  included  as  follows:  no  antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, or benzodiazepines were taken in two weeks prior to hospitalization; no electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), or other forms of physical therapies and psychotherapy within the last  six  months.  The  exclusion  criteria  for  patients  include:  (1)  previous  or  current history  of  other  psychiatric  disorders;  (2)  alcohol  dependence  or  use  of  other psychoactive  substances;  (3)  secondary  depressive  episode  resulting  from  the  brain organic psychosis or other disorders; (4) MRI scan contraindications; (5) women who are pregnant, lactating, or plan to become pregnant. The exclusion criteria for healthy controls  were  similar  to  those  for  patients. Additionally,  the  healthy  subjects,  who included current or past psychiatric or neurological disorders and any history of mental illness in first-degree relatives, were left out.

All subjects underwent MRI scans before treatment. Among them, 187 depressed patients received SSRIs (including escitalopram, fluvoxamine, sertraline, paroxetine, and fluoxetine) or SNRIs (including venlafaxine and duloxetine) treatment based on the patient's  symptoms  and  the  recommendation  of  professional  psychiatrists.  Over  the

following four-week treatment period, 154 patients did not receive electroconvulsive therapy, cognitive and behavioral therapy, or any other physical therapy. The dosage of antidepressants followed the latest Chinese depression treatment guidelines: escitalopram 10-20 mg/day, fluvoxamine 100-300 mg/day, sertraline 100-200 mg/day, paroxetine  20-60  mg/day,  fluoxetine  20-60  mg/day,  venlafaxine  150-225  mg/day, duloxetine  60-120  mg/day.  In  this  study,  patients  with  at  least  a  50%  reduction  in HAMD-17 total scores after four weeks were conventionally defined as responders ( n = 111; 77 with SSRIs, 34 with SNRIs) and patients with less than 50% were defined as non-responders ( n = 43; 35 with SSRIs, 8 with SNRIs).

This retrospective study was approved by the Research Ethics Review Board of NBH  and  NDTH. All  participants  signed  written  informed  consent  after  complete written and oral explanations.

## MRI acquisition and preprocessing

Images of Datasets I-III were all acquired on Siemens scanners and further details about MRI acquisition was shown in Table S1. For Datasets I and II, all structural MRI images were preprocessed with default parameter setting in CAT12 toolkit (http://dbm.neuro.uni-jena.de/cat/) implemented in SPM12 (https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/spm/software/spm12/). The processing pipeline including bias-corrected, tissue classified, and normalized data ratings; skull stripping; segmentation into whiter matter, gray matter and cerebrospinal fluid; normalization to Montreal  Neurological  Institute  (MNI)  space  to  a  1.5  mm  isotropic  adult  template provided  by  CAT12.  The  DARTEL  (diffeomorphic  anatomical  registration  using exponentiated Lie algebra) algorithm was applied for segmentation and normalization processes. The resulting gray matter images were spatially smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of 8-mm full-width at half-maximum.

Dataset III from REST-meta-MDD Project only provides processed data rather than raw data. All the image data were preprocessed using DPARSF (http://rfmri.org/DPARSF) based on SPM. Similarly, the segmented gray matter images were smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of 8-mm full-width at half-maximum.

## Description of statistical methods

The two-sample t-test and Mann-Whitney U test are both statistical tests used to compare two groups or samples. The t-test is appropriate when the data follow a normal distribution  and  the  variances  of  the  two  groups  are  assumed  to  be  equal  (or approximately equal). It is used to assess whether there is a significant difference in the means of two independent groups. On the other hand, the Mann-Whitney U test, also known as the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, is a non-parametric test that does not assume a specific distribution for the data. It is used when the data do not meet the assumptions of the t-test, such as when the data are skewed or the variances of the groups are unequal. The  Mann-Whitney  U  test  assesses  whether  there  is  a  significant  difference  in  the medians of two independent groups.

## Latent Dirichlet Analysis

The Latent Dirichlet Analysis (LDA) model was originally developed to discover latent topics automatically in a corpus of text documents, which consists of a three-tier structure of words, potential topics and documents. The LDA model assumes that each word in a text document is obtained through the process of "selecting a topic with a certain probability (Pr(topic|document)), and selecting a word with a certain probability from the  topic  (Pr(word|topic))",  and  finally  realizes  the  purpose  of  identifying  the latent  topics.  LDA  allows  a  document  to  be  associated  with  multiple  latent  topics (shared  across  documents),  and  each  topic  can  be  associated  with  multiple  words (shared  across  topics).  In  addition,  LDA  adopts  the  word  bag  model,  which  only consider whether a word appears or not, regardless of the order. This is consistent with our MRI data, because the damage of each brain region is disordered.

LDA is estimated based on how often a dictionary word appears in the document. To map the z -scores obtained from normative model (range, [-5, 5]) to the LDA model, one  can  think  of  each  patients'  individual  normative  probability  maps  as  a  text document, brain regions as dictionary words and the latent lesion factors as topics. In order to correspond brain region measures to dictionary words, and also to meet the

input  requirements  of  the  LDA  model,  the z -scores  from  normative  models  were multiplied by 10 and rounded to the nearest integer. Although the discretization of z -scores  might  lead  to  some  loss  of  information,  we  note  that  a  sufficiently  large multiplicative factor can minimize the loss of information (see Supplemental Results). Thus, the greater abnormal degree corresponded to the larger counts of word. However, traditional LDA can only input positive frequency of dictionary words, which does not apply to our MRI data. The patients are associated with both atrophy and expansion of brain,  and  the  negative  (or  positive)  values  cannot  simply  be  ignored  or  taken  as absolute values. Thus, in this study, we used a variant of the LDA model, polar LDA (1), which adds a binary variable to indicate whether there was hyper-GMV or hypoGMV for each region.

The discrete zscores within two different subdivided patient populations (SSRI: responder vs. non-responder; responder: SSRIs vs. SNRIs) were fed into the polar LDA model to estimate the latent factors. We chose factor number K = 2-5 to estimate the reasonable setting. Solutions were calculated 100 times from 100 random initializations, where  the  variational  expectation-maximization  algorithm  was  applied  to  estimate Pr(Factor|Patient) and E(GMV patterns|Factor) under each setting of K (1, 2).

## Supplemental Results

The generality of normative models

To  verify  the  generality  of  normative  models,  the  HCs  in  discovery  cohort  of Dataset I did not participant in normative modeling, but were directly applied to obtain the deviations from the normal distribution constructed via large samples from Dataset II&amp;III. Results showed that more than 95% of HCs were within the normative range for the majority of brain regions (Figure S2A), confirming that the normative models were not predicting an excessive number of deviations (i.e., more than 95% of individuals were within the 2.5% to 97.5% confidence interval).

Among these regional normative models, the correlations between the predicted and true value in the training data (Dataset II&amp;III) were low (less than 0.5) in bilateral thalamus, which were not included in subsequent analysis.

## 2) 5 × 2 cross-validation t/F-test:

## LDA Control Analysis

To  test  whether  the  current  multiplication  factor  (10  in  this  study)  would  lose information, we tried to re-estimate the latent factors in one of the subdivided patient

populations (SSRI: responder vs. non-responder) with multiplication factors of 100, 1000 and 10000, respectively, and conduct correlation analysis between factors and factor expression. The results showed that although the correlations between the factors decreases with the increase of the multiplication factors (Figure S8A), which means that there is indeed some information loss, but the correlations between the factors were very high (correlation greater than 0.9985). We then compared the expression of the factor with the penalty factor of 10,000 with the multiplication factor of 10. The result showed that the factor composition of patients are similar both in distribution (Figure S8B) and correlation analysis (Figure S8C). Therefore, we could conclude that there is essentially no loss of information using the current multiplication factor.
