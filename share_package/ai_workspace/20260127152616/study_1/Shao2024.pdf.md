## Archival Report

## Capturing the Individual Deviations From Normative Models of Brain Structure for Depression Diagnosis and Treatment

Junneng Shao, Jiaolong Qin, Huan Wang, Yurong Sun, Wei Zhang, Xinyi Wang, Ting Wang, Li Xue, Zhijian Yao, and Qing Lu

## ABSTRACT

BACKGROUND: The high heterogeneity of depression prevents us from obtaining reproducible and de /uniFB01 nite anatomical maps of brain structural changes associated with the disorder, which limits the individualized diagnosis and treatment of patients. In this study, we investigated the clinical issues related to depression according to individual deviations from normative ranges of gray matter volume.

METHODS: We enrolled 1092 participants, including 187 patients with depression and 905 healthy control participants. Structural magnetic resonance imaging data of healthy control participants from the Human Connectome Project ( n = 510) and REST-meta-MDD Project ( n = 229) were used to establish a normative model across the life span in adults 18 to 65 years old for each brain region. Deviations from the normative range for 187 patients and 166 healthy control participants recruited from two local hospitals were captured as normative probability maps, which were used to identify the disease risk and treatment-related latent factors.

RESULTS: In contrast to case-control results, our normative modeling approach revealed highly individualized patterns of anatomic abnormalities in depressed patients (less than 11% extreme deviation overlapping for any regions). Based on our classi /uniFB01 cation framework, models trained with individual normative probability maps (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve range, 0.7146 -0.7836) showed better performance than models trained with original gray matter volume values (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve range, 0.6800 -0.7036), which was veri /uniFB01 ed in an independent external test set. Furthermore, different latent brain structural factors in relation to antidepressant treatment were revealed by a Bayesian model based on normative probability maps, suggesting distinct treatment response and inclination.

CONCLUSIONS: Capturing personalized deviations from a normative range could help in understanding the heterogeneous neurobiology of depression and thus guide clinical diagnosis and treatment of depression.

Depression is one of the most prevalent psychiatric disorders, with prevalence showing a predominant increasing trend over time (1,2). According to the World Health Organization, depression is the single largest contributor to global disability, affecting more than 300 million people yearly (3), with a lifetime risk of 15% to 18% (4). The current gold standard for diagnosis and treatment outcomes in depression mainly depends on the criteria from DSM; however, the classi /uniFB01 cation in DSM is not clear, and clinical subjectivity easily causes confusion (5). Moreover, based on the current prevailing treatment guidelines for antidepressant choice, only approximately 50% of patients with depression respond to /uniFB01 rst-line treatment (6). Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop effective and objective neurobiological markers to help guide the diagnosis and treatment of depression in individual patients.

As a noninvasive measurement, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been widely used in the study of pathophysiology in depression, providing new opportunities to develop

precise and personalized diagnosis and prognosis of depression (7,8). However, even though the brain structure is relatively stable, the results were inconsistent across studies when considering only decreased gray matter volume (GMV) in patients with depression compared with control participants (9,10). This might be partly explained by two factors. First, depression is a highly heterogeneous psychiatric syndrome (11 -13). DSM identi /uniFB01 es at least 256 unique symptom manifestations that meet the standard de /uniFB01 nition of depression, resulting in extensive clinical heterogeneity (14). Further, patients vary greatly in the temporal dynamics of symptom onset, including age of onset and frequency of episodes. Depression is also heterogeneous in psychiatric comorbidities and treatment outcomes (11,15). Second, the use of relatively small samples might lead to type I and II errors (16), therefore limiting the generalizability of results. These factors are inevitable challenges in the process of achieving precision medicine in depression.

The goal of precision medicine is to provide timely and accurate diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment for individual patients (17 -19). However, traditional case-control analyses allow only statistical inferences at the group level, rather than the individual level, and cannot fully capture brain abnormalities at the individual level (20,21). Furthermore, most studies employ standard single-variable analysis methods, the predictive power of which, either singularly or cumulatively, remains inadequate (22). Machine learning offers a new paradigm that enables automated and more objective assessment for prognosis and diagnosis of depression. Studies have shown that machine learning models could predict treatment-resistant depression with comparable accuracy (0.7) (23). Besides, data-driven subtypes of depression can facilitate an understanding of heterogeneity, which may help improve the predictability of diagnosis and clinical outcomes (24,25). Therefore, an approach that takes into account individual heterogeneity combined with machine learning algorithms is needed to understand the pathophysiological changes of depression and to explore whether there are potential diagnostic and antidepressant treatment-related effects.

Normative modeling is an emerging statistical technique for mapping individual deviations at the participant level with respect to a reference model, which could help in understanding the heterogeneous biology underlying psychiatric disorders (26,27). In this study, a large dataset of healthy adults 18 to 65 years of age from the Human Connectome Project (28) and REST-meta-MDD Project (29) was used to estimate normative models of GMV across the life span. These models were then applied to patient cohorts to assess the individual deviations from a normative range. Based on this, we developed three applications, as follows: 1) We built an individual depression prediction model in a discovery cohort to mine the depression-related information over those deviations from the normative range and provide an individualized objective assessment. We also explored the performance of the prediction model in an independent external test set. 2) A Bayesian model was used to explore the brain structural changes associated with antidepressant treatment to /uniFB01 nd the latent brain factors in the subgroup of patients who are sensitive to treatment with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs). 3) We further explored the similarities and differences in the brain structure of patients targeted by two typical antidepressants, i.e., SSRIs and serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs). This study provides new insights into the neurobiological heterogeneity of depression and demonstrates its promising application in clinical diagnosis and treatment.

## METHODS AND MATERIALS

## Participants

The cohorts of this study were mainly composed of three datasets, two of which were from an open data sharing project (details of participant inclusion in Figure S1). In dataset I, we enrolled 127 depressed patients and 110 healthy control (HC) participants from the Af /uniFB01 liated Brain Hospital of Nanjing Medical University as the discovery cohort. Additionally, 60 patients and 56 HC participants from Nanjing Drum Tower

Hospital were recruited as an external test set. Details of this dataset and treatment of patients are provided in Supplemental Methods. In this study, patients with at least a 50% reduction in the total scores of 17-item Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression after 4 weeks were conventionally de /uniFB01 ned as responders ( n = 111; 77 with SSRIs, 34 with SNRIs), and patients with less than 50% reduction were de /uniFB01 ned as nonresponders ( n = 43; 35 with SSRIs, 8 with SNRIs).

To construct the normative models, we downloaded phenotypic data and MRI T1-weighted data from the Human Connectome Project (dataset II; 510 HC participants; age range, 22 -36 years) (28) and REST-meta-MDD Project (dataset III; 229 HC participants; age range, 18 -65 years; site 20) (29). All images in datasets I through III were acquired on Siemens 3T MRI scanners (Siemens Healthineers). Further details about MRI acquisition and preprocessed pipeline are provided in Table S1 and Supplemental Methods. After all T1 images were preprocessed, the GMV of each brain region was estimated based on the Automated Anatomical Labeling atlas, and the total intracranial volume (TIV) for each participant was calculated simultaneously.

## Statistical Analysis

Continuous clinical variables were compared between patients with depression and HC participants in dataset I using the twosample t test or Mann-Whitney U test after normality testing (Supplemental Methods). The c 2 test was used to compare categorical clinical variables. Regional GMV between patients and HC participants was examined using the two-sample t test after regressing out several covariates, including age, sex, and TIV, to avoid potential confounders. Two-tailed p , .05 indicated a signi /uniFB01 cant statistical difference when false discovery rate was used for multiple comparison correction.

## Harmonization

To minimize the nonbiological variance introduced by differences in MRI scanners and acquisition protocols, we used the ComBat method (30) to harmonize data from these three datasets. ComBat is a popular batch adjustment method developed for genomics expression analysis that has been demonstrated to remove the unwanted variation introduced by site in diffusion tensor imaging (31,32) or structural MRI (33) studies, while effectively preserving biological variability. Speci /uniFB01 cally, we /uniFB01 rst harmonized the data from dataset I (discovery cohort) and datasets II and III into a common domain using the ComBat method. To avoid a potential data leakage problem for subsequent classi /uniFB01 cation modeling, we retained the biological variability related to age, sex, and TIV, without preserving the diagnostic labels. Then, the pretrained ComBat model was applied to harmonize the external test set directly (Figure 1A).

## Depression Prediction and Performance Validation

According to the NPMs obtained from the normative models, depression evaluation models were trained over the discovery cohort in dataset I for individual prediction. First, the enrolled participants in the discovery cohort were randomly divided into a training set and a test set in a 4:1 ratio. To avoid the in /uniFB02 uence of data splitting, we randomly repeated dividing training and test sets with the above proportion 10,000 times independently (40). In different splits of sample, the proportion of patients and HC participants remained consistent in both the training (102 patients vs. 88 HC participants) and the test (25 patients vs. 22 HC participants) sets. Moreover, feature selection was performed using max-relevance and minredundancy (41) with 10-fold cross-validation. Then, each trained model was used to predict the diagnostic category for each participant in the corresponding test set, which was rebuilt and tested during the 10,000 resampling procedure.

To verify the superiority of individual GMV deviation measure over large sample normative distribution, we also constructed depression prediction models with original GMV as performance comparison. The related aspects, including the effect of sites harmonization, kernels for support vector machine (linear, sigmoid, and radial basis function), and feature dimension, were comprehensively analyzed. Finally, each model trained in the discovery cohort was applied to the external test set to evaluate the generalizability and transportability of the models under different sites (Figure 1C, left).

## Treatment-Related Analysis of Patients With Depression

An unsupervised Bayesian model, polar Latent Dirichlet Allocation (pLDA) (42,43), was applied to identify latent factors associated with treatment in patients with depression based on the NPMs. To apply this model to our GMV data, the z scores from normative models were multiplied by 10 and rounded to the nearest integer (details in Supplemental Methods). Given the discretized z scores and the number of prede /uniFB01 ned factors, K , we can estimate the GMV patterns of factor speci /uniFB01 city [i.e., E(GMV patterns|Factor)] and the factor composition of each patient [i.e., Pr(Factor|Patient)].

First, as most patients in our study were treated with SSRIs, we applied one pLDA model to the patients treated with SSRIs

(division I: 77 responders treated with SSRIs, 35 nonresponders treated with SSRIs) to explore different bias of brain structural patterns in patients who respond or not to SSRIs (Figure 1C, middle). Second, for the patients with treatment response, we applied the other pLDA to identify homogeneity and heterogeneity in patients who had different inclination to these two typical antidepressants (division II: 77 responders treated with SSRIs, 34 responders treated with SNRIs) (Figure 1C, right). We chose the factor number K = 2 -5 to estimate the reasonable setting. Under each setting of K (each for 100 random initializations), the variational expectation maximization algorithm was adopted to estimated Pr(Factor|Patient) and E(GMV patterns|Factor).

## Detection of Patients With Depression

The predicted diagnostic probability obtained from the depression prediction modeling for each individual was used to calculate the area under the receiver operating characteristic curves (AUCs) for each depression prediction model. Due to the skewed distribution of the AUCs in some prediction models, we calculated the median (Figure 3) and the lower and upper 2.5th percentile (Table S6) of the 10,000 AUCs as a performance measure for each model. In the discovery cohort, the prediction models with feature selection training consistently showed better performance than models without feature selection under any conditions. Thus, we mainly focused on the models trained after feature selection.

As shown in Figure 3, the prediction models trained by NPMs always performed better (AUC range, 0.7146 -0.7836 vs. 0.6800 -0.7036), which revealed the validity and rationality of normative modeling. Furthermore, except for the relatively poor linear support vector machine models, the performance of models was improved after harmonization with the ComBat method. Similar results were found in the external test set: the prediction models based on normative modeling showed relatively good performance (AUC range, 0.6063 -0.7396), while those trained with the regional GMV showed only average performances (AUC range, 0.5613 -0.6089). Furthermore, we performed a statistical comparison of model performance for the best model (radial basis function kernel), and the results of this statistical analysis support our view above: the AUC is higher when using normative deviations compared with the original GMV values (see details in Supplemental Results and Figure S3).

## DISCUSSION

Without consideration of individual heterogeneity, group comparison results associated with depression have limited values in personal clinical evaluation. Hence, in this study, we aimed to dissect the heterogeneous neurobiology of patients with depression by mapping the regionwise deviations of each individual participant from normative models of GMV development based on a large healthy cohort. In contrast to results suggested by the traditional case-control study approach, our normative modeling approach tried to dissect individualized patterns of anatomic abnormalities in relation to depression. Although the proportion of depressed patients with extreme deviations in each brain region was obviously higher than that of HC participants, no single brain region was found for which the majority of patients deviated from the normative range. Moreover, we found that the individualized deviations from normative range had great potential to guide the diagnosis and treatment (medication strategies) of depression.

The normative model describes the developmental variation in typical brain, with a largely monotonic decrease in GMV throughout development, consistent with previous neuroimaging studies (44 -46). Gray matter abnormalities are indeed a core neuropathological feature of depression (9). Some relatively large neuroimaging meta-analyses performed to date have yielded a neuropathological pro /uniFB01 le of a disorder associated with profound structural changes, which involved mainly prefrontal and limbic regions (47 -49) (with varying effect sizes) as well as parts of parietal and temporal lobes (9,50). However, we observed widespread individual differences between depressed participants in terms of their deviations from normative range to some extent, while the group-average maps of depression pathology fail to be accurately represented for individual prediction. This /uniFB01 nding is in accordance with the notion that psychiatric disorders are complex, with few common genetic variations, brain structural defects, or environmental stressors (51). Depression may be driven by different neurophysiological mechanisms, leading to different symptom characteristics, clinical trajectories, and treatment outcomes (11). Thus, applying the traditional case-control inferential paradigm to the current broad diagnostic constructs may not precisely pin down depression-related changes in GMV to distinct cortical regions (33,52).

From a methodological perspective, normative modeling provides an alternative conceptual framework for understanding the heterogeneous neurobiology of depression based on deviations from the typical patterns. Similarly, using the idea of normative range, the prediction of brain age is a reverse estimation method (53,54). Published studies comparing the estimated age with the true age of patients

## Conclusions

We estimated a normative model of brain development based on a large cohort of HC participants and /uniFB01 rst applied this model to depression-related clinical practice problems. Our results suggested that the loci of depression-related changes in GMV differ vastly among patients. Normative models may provide an alternative to a case-control paradigm that tests hypotheses at the group level and thus contribute to understanding the heterogeneous neurobiology of depression. Our study con /uniFB01 rmed that capturing these personalized deviations from a normative range could help diagnose depression at an individual level and provide guidance on treatment options for patients. In the future, individual deviation maps could also be used to delineate more putative biological subtypes or provide more detailed personalized treatment options and thus move toward precision medicine.
