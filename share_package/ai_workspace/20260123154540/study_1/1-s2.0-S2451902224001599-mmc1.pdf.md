## Study 1 Participant Details

Clinical high-risk for psychosis (CHR-P) and early illness schizophrenia (ESZ) individuals were recruited from the early psychosis research clinic at University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). CHR-P participants met Criteria of Psychosis-Risk Syndromes (COPS) criteria as determined by the Structured Interview for Psychosis-Risk Syndromes (SIPS) (1-3). ESZ individuals ( n =78) met criteria for a current diagnosis of schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder as determined by the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-IV (SCID) (4). Healthy control (HC) individuals were recruited from the local UCSF community and did not meet Axis I diagnostic criteria as determined by the SCID 4 (for HC &gt;16 years old) or the Kiddie Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia for School-Age Children, Present and Lifetime Version (for HC ≤ 16 years old) (5). All participants were required to be in good physical health, fluent in English, and between 12-35 years old. Participants were excluded if they had a past year DSMIV substance dependence diagnosis (excluding nicotine), or head injury with loss of consciousness, neurological illness.

## Study 2 Participant Details

HC, non-affective psychosis (NAff-P), and affective psychosis (Aff-P) individuals were scanned at one of three sites (Indiana University [ n =80], Brigham and Women's Hospital [ n =46], and McLean Hospital [ n =43]) as part of the Human Connectome Project-Early Psychosis (HCPEP) study. Diagnoses were determined by the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM5 -RV (SCID-5-RV) (6). NAff-P individuals ( n =83) met DSM-5   (6) criteria for schizophrenia ( n =58), schizophreniform ( n =9), schizoaffective ( n =11), psychosis not otherwise specified ( n =3), delusional disorder ( n =1), or brief psychotic disorder ( n =1). Aff-P participants ( n =30) met DSM-

Inclusion criteria included: 1) 16-35 years old, 2) outpatients, 3) male or female, 4) fluent in English, 5) not pregnant, and 6) willing to share de-identified data. HC individuals had no history of psychiatric hospitalization and were not taking psychiatric medications.

## Structural MRI Acquisition

Study 1 participants were scanned at the University of California San Francisco's (UCSF) neuroimaging center using a Siemens 3T TIM TRIO scanner and high-resolution structural T1weighted MPRAGE image acquisition sequence: repetition time (TR)=2,300 ms, echo time (TE)=2.95 ms, flip angle= 9˚, field of view (FOV) =256mm, and slice thickness=1.20 mm.

Study 2 participants were scanned at different HCP-EP sites using a Siemens Prisma 3T MRI scanner and identical scan sequences: TR=2,400ms, TE=2.22ms, FOV=256mm, and slice thickness=0.80mm. To achieve harmonization of brain scan data across sites, all sites used a Siemens Prisma 3T MRI scanner, implemented identical scan sequences and protocols, and did not upgrade scanner software or hardware during the study period. Further, MRI phantoms were scanned repeatedly at each site, including at the beginning and the end of the study, to monitor MRI sequence stability over the study period. Sites were trained by four study staff who traveled

to two or three of the sites and underwent the scan protocol. Scan data were inspected to ensure that scans from the same individual looked similar between sites. However, these travelling scans were not used to derive quantitative correction factors to harmonize data.

## Z -Scoring Normalization Formulas

Age, gender, site (for Study 2), and global thickness (for global thickness model) effects for each ROI were modeled in the HC group using this formula:

y i = β 0+ β 1*age + β 2*gender + β 3*site (for Study 2) + β 4*global thickness (for global thickness model) + e i .

Regional cortical thickness values were transformed to adjusted z -scores by subtracting predicted values from observed values and then dividing by the standard error of regression from the HC regression model using this formula:

z -score = observed cortical thickness value-predicted normal value root mean square error ( RMSE )

## Study 1 Intracranial Volume (ICV) and Regional Thickness Associations Sensitivity Results

For most models regressing raw ROI thickness on raw ICV and Group, the Group x ICV interaction term was not significant and was dropped. Majority of common slope tests between ROI thickness and ICV were not significant ( p s&gt;.052, d =0.00 to 0.26). Two ROI (rostral middle frontal, pars orbitalis) models showed significant Group x ICV interactions, indicating betweengroup slope differences (see Table S5 for group slope comparisons). When models were run separately by group, ROI thickness and ICV were negatively correlated in CHR-P ( r s=-.42 to -.32; p s&lt;.024), but not in HC ( r s=.-10 to -.06; p s&gt;.366) or ESZ ( r s=.08-.12; p s&gt;.301). For 29 of the other 32 regions, ROI thickness and ICV were not significantly associated. For the other 3

regions (medial orbitofrontal, caudal anterior cingulate, posterior cingulate), common slope tests showed direct relationships between ROI thickness and ICV ( p s&lt;.010, Cohen's d =.35-.51).

## Study 1 Regional Thickness Adjusted for Intracranial Volume (ICV) Sensitivity Results

Norm-adjusted cortical thickness z -scores with ICV adjustment closely resembled nonICV-adjusted profiles. ICV-adjusted ROI profiles among HC, CHR-P, and ESZ groups (Figure S1a left panel) showed significant non-parallelism ( F [66,368]=1.80, Wilks' λ =.57, p &lt;.001, d =1.13). In 2-group follow-up comparisons, the ESZ profile was non-parallel relative to the flat HC profile ( F [33,134]=2.00, Wilks' λ =.67, p =.003, d =1.40) and CHR-P profile ( F [33,95]=1.65, Wilks' λ =.64, p =.032, d =1.51). Non-parallelism of ESZ profile, relative to HC and CHR profiles, reflected significant thinning across many ROIs, with prominent thinning in superior frontal, inferior parietal, and banks of the superior temporal sulcus, but normal thickness in caudal anterior cingulate, temporal pole, entorhinal, parahippocampal, and pericalcarine. In terms of level differences, a significant Group effect emerged ( F [2,216]=17.44, p &lt;.001, d =0.80), with pairwise follow-up tests showing ESZ profile to be reduced in thickness relative to HC ( F [1,166]=35.28, p &lt;.001, d =0.92) and CHR-P profiles ( F [1,127]=9.95, p =.002, d =0.56). In contrast to analyses without ICV adjust, ICV-adjusted results showed a significant parallelism different between HC and CHR-P ( F [33,107]=1.80, Wilks' λ =.64, p =.013, d =1.49) but did not differ in level ( F [1,139]=3.32, p =.071, d =0.31).

## Study 2 ICV and Regional Thickness Associations Sensitivity Results

For most models regressing raw ROI thickness on raw ICV and Group, the Group x ICV interaction term was not significant and was dropped. The majority of common slope tests of the relationship between ROI thickness and ICV were not significant ( p s&gt;.060, d =0.00-0.29). Five ROI (rostral middle frontal, lateral occipital, lingual, cuneus, pericalcarine) models showed significant Group x ICV interactions, indicating between-group slope differences (see Table S6 for group slope comparisons). When models were run separately by group, ROI thickness and ICV were not significantly associated, but the slope differences arose from non-significant negative slopes in CHR-P ( r s=-.21 to .00; p s&gt;.136) and non-significant positive slopes in HC and ESZ ( r s=-.12 to .14; p s&gt;.228). For 23 of the other 29 regions, where no significant interactions by Group emerged, the common slopes of the ROI thickness relationships with ICV were not significant. And for the other 6 regions (pars opercularis, caudal anterior cingulate, superior temporal, middle temporal, inferior temporal, banks of the superior temporal sulcus), common slope tests showed direct relationships between ROI thickness and ICV ( p s&lt;.030, Cohen's d =0.06-0.34).

## Study 2 Regional Thickness Adjusted for Intracranial Volume (ICV) Sensitivity Results

Norm-adjusted cortical thickness z -scores with ICV adjustment closely resembled nonICV-adjusted profiles. ICV-adjusted ROI profiles among HC, NAff-P, and Aff-P groups (Figure S1c left panel) showed significant non-parallelism ( F [66,268]=1.65, Wilks' λ =.51, p =.003, d =1.28) and level differences ( F [2,166]=17.35, p &lt;.001, d =1.03). In 2-group follow-up comparisons, NAff-P profile was non-parallel relative to the flat HC profile ( F [33,105]=2.53, Wilks' λ =.56, p &lt;.001, d =1.44), with all but four ROIs (pars orbitalis, paracentral, entorhinal, temporal pole) showing thickness deficits and the largest deficits evident in middle and inferior temporal gyri, insula, and supramarginal gyrus. NAff-P also showed a level reduction (i.e.,

widespread thinning) relative to HC ( F [1,137]=30.99, p &lt;.001, d =0.95). Unlike non-adjusted regional profiles, Aff-P profile was parallel to HC ( F [33,52]=1.26, Wilks' λ =.56, p =.224, d =1.79), and there was no level difference between HC and Aff-P ( F [1,84]=0.86, p =.356, d =0.21. Comparison of NAff-P and Aff-P showed their ROI profiles to be parallel ( F [33,79] =1.22, Wilks' λ =.66, p =.234, d =1.43) but with a significant level difference ( F [1,111]=11.34, p &lt;.001, d =0.64), with NAff-P having a thinner cortex.
