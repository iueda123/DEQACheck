**Supplementary Methods**

**Clinical assessments:**

**Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised (ADI-R)**

The ADI-R is a standardized, comprehensive, semi-structured, investigator-based interview of the caregivers [1]. It covers most developmental and behavioral aspects of autism spectrum disorder (ASD), including evaluating reciprocally social interaction, communication, repetitive behaviors, and stereotyped patterns for children with a mental age from about 18 months into adulthood. The Chinese version of the ADI-R was approved by the World Psychological Association in 2007 [2]. All the interviewers reached an agreement over 90%, ranging from 98.25±1.91 to 99.38±1.06 against the rating of each item in the Chinese ADI-R by qualified ADI-R trainers (SS Gau and YY Wu) before the implementation of this study. The Chinese version of the ADI-R has been widely used in autism research in Taiwan (e.g., [3-5]).

**Kiddie epidemiologic version of the schedule for affective disorders and Schizophrenia (K-SADS-E) and SADS**

The K-SADS-E [6] is a standard structured tool to assess current and previous mental disorders for youths from 6 to 18 years old [7, 8], while its original form (SADS) was developed for adults aged 18 and older [9, 10]. The Chinese version of K-SADS-E has been widely applied in several studies to screen for current and lifetime mental disorders in clinical, neuropsychological, and neuroimage research, e.g., [11-16]. In this study, we used the Chinese K-SADS-E and modified adult ADHD supplement of K-SADS-E to confirm the diagnoses of ADHD for participants aged 18 years old and younger and those more aged than 18, respectively, for the ADHD group. The Chinese K-SADS-E and SADS as well as for all the participants to ensure that none of them had any current or previous mental disorders, including schizophrenic disorders, affective disorders, anxiety disorders, and childhood-onset mental disorders such as ASD (only for the ASD group), ADHD (only for the ADHD group), oppositional disorder, etc. for the typically developing controls.

**Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS)**

The PANSS [17] includes 7 items on positive symptoms, 7 items on negative symptoms, and 16 items on general symptoms. Psychiatrists made the ratings on a 7 point Likert scale, with the higher score representing the higher severity. Thus, we summed the 7 positive symptom items for positive subscores, 7 negative symptom items for negative subscores, and 16 general symptom items for general subscores. The psychometric properties of the Chinese version of the PANSS have been established [18], and it has been widely used in schizophrenic research in ethnic Han Chinese populations [19, 20].

**Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS)**

The SRS is a self- or caregiver-reported four-point Likert-type questionnaire for quantifying autistic traits based on the frequency of each behavior ("0" never true and "3" always true) [21]. It consists of 65 items, covering the descriptions of social awareness, social cognition, social communication, social motivation, and autistic mannerisms. It has good reliability and concurrent validity relative to the ADI-R [22, 23]. The psychometric properties of its Chinese version have been established with a validated four-factor structure, i.e., social awareness, social communication, social emotion, and stereotyped behaviors [24], and has been widely used in autism-related research in Taiwan, e.g., [25-28]. We summed up the items within the same factor [24], and used the four subscores to correlate with neural tract integrity.

**Swanson, Nolan, and Pelham, version IV scale (SNAP-IV)**

The ADHD and oppositional defiant symptoms were reported by parents on the Chinese version of the SNAP-IV [29, 30], which is a 26-item scale rating on a 4-point Likert scale with a score of 0 for "not at all," 1 for "just a little," 2 for "quite a bit," and 3 for "very much." There are 18 items parallel to the core symptoms of DSM-IV ADHD (Items 1-9 for inattention symptoms; Items 10-18 for hyperactivity/impulsivity symptoms), and 8 items based on the DSM-IV of oppositional defiant disorder (ODD) symptoms. The psychometric properties and norm of the Chinese SNAP IV-Parent form have been established in Taiwan [30], showing the same 3-factor structure as its English version. It has been used in many clinic-based [31-34] and community-based (e.g., [8, 35-38]) studies in Taiwan

**Wechsler Intelligence Scale**

Two versions of the Wechsler Intelligence Scale were used to assess the intelligence level of all the participants according to the age of the assessment. The *Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale-III* [39] was used for subjects older than 16 years, while the *Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children-III [40]* was used aged 6-16 years. The subtests analyzed in this study included digit symbol (proposed to measure processing speed), block design (proposed to measure visual-spatial processing and problem-solving; visual-motor construction), digit span (backward and forward) (proposed to measure working memory, attention, encoding, auditory processing), arithmetic (proposed to measure quantitative reasoning, concentration, mental manipulation), similarity (abstract verbal reasoning; semantic knowledge), and information (degree of general information acquired from culture), as well as working memory index (computed by digit span and arithmetic).

**Cambridge Neuropsychological Test Automated Batteries (CANTAB)**

The CANTAB is a set of computerized tests to examine nonverbal neuropsychological functions (CANTABeclipseTH: Test Administration Guide version 3). We selected Motor Screening Task (MOT), Reaction Time (RTI), Rapid Visual Information Processing (RVP), Spatial Working Memory (SWM), Intra-Extra Dimensional Set Shift (IED), One-Touch Stockings of Cambridge (OTS), and Paired Associates Learning (PAL) for this study. Our group has published more than 20 papers using the CANTAB to assess a wide range of neuropsychological functions in individuals with mental disorders (e.g., [10, 41, 42]).

***MOT***

The MOT provides a general assessment of whether sensorimotor deficits or lack of comprehension will limit the collection of valid data from the participant. Colored crosses are presented in different locations on the screen, one at a time. The participant must select the cross on the screen as quickly and accurately as possible. Outcome measures assess the participant's speed of response and the accuracy of pointing (selecting the cross).

***RTI***

The RTI is designed to measure the participant's speed of response to a visual target where the stimulus is either predictable (simple reaction time) or unpredictable (choice reaction time). Five successive stages have increasingly complex response requirements. Firstly, the participant had to touch the screen when a spot appeared on the screen. Next, the spot may appear in any one of five locations. Then, the participant was required to hold down the press button until the spot appeared on the screen but did not have to touch the screen. In the 4th stage, the participant was required to hold down the press button until the spot appeared and touch the place where the spot appeared. In the final stage, the participant had to hold down the press button until the spot appeared, then release the button and touch the position where the spot was presented.

***RVP***

The RVP task, a 4-min visual continuous performance task modified from Wesnes and Warburton's task (1984), is designed to assess sustained attention capacity [43]. Digits (ranging from 2 to 9) appeared in the screen center in random order, one at a time (100 digits/min). Participants were asked to press a response pad when they detected any one of three number sequences (3–5–7, 2–4–6, or 4–6–8). The following indices were presented: (1) total misses (occasions the participant failed to respond); (2) probability of hits (correct response, *h* ), i.e., total hits divided by the sum of total hits and total misses; (3) total correct rejections (stimuli that were correctly rejected); (4) probability of false alarms (the participant responding incorrectly, *f* ), i.e., total false alarms divided by the sum of total false alarms and total correct rejections [44]; (5) A' (calculated as 0.5+[( *h* − *f* )+( *h* − *f* )2]/[4× *h* ×(1− *f* )]), a signal detection measure of sensitivity to the target, regardless of response tendency [45]; and (6) mean latency (mean time taken to respond in the correct responses). Sensitivity (A') refers to how hard or easy it is to detect the presence of a target stimulus from background events. This index of target sensitivity is considered to represent attentiveness [46].

SWM

Each box on the screen contains only one token per trial. Participants were instructed to search through boxes to find sufficient blue tokens hidden inside. To complete trials in minimum steps, participants should remember the boxes which had already shown blue tokens. The SWM task begins with 4 practice trials (3-box problem), followed by formal trials (4-, 6-, and 8-box problems). The test results consist of 3 types of errors: "within error" occurs when participants search the same box more than once in the same trial; "between error" represents a mistake in searching boxes that have already shown blue tokens; and "double error" can be categorized as both a within error and a between error. We used total errors to represent spatial working memory deficits.

***IED***

The IED is designed to evaluate mental flexibility. There are stimuli composed of unfamiliar pink shapes and white lines in the task. Participants are instructed to select one of the two stimuli freely in the beginning. After they make the decision, the screen gives them feedback to assist in forming particular rules. The rules would be changed when participants had selected the correct stimuli for 6 consecutive trials. To complete the task, participants had to pass through 9 stages. The task ended if participants failed to select correct stimuli for 50 consecutive trials. The following indexes were selected: (1) pre-extradimensional shift errors: participants selected the correct stimuli depending on the previously ignored dimension of stimuli; and (2) the number of completed stage trials.

***OTS***

The OTS is a spatial planning test variant based upon the CANTAB Stockings of Cambridge test. The screen shows two displays containing three colored balls. The participant was asked to use the balls in the lower display to copy the pattern shown in the upper display. The complexity of the problems increases from one move to four moves. If the participant made too many moves to solve the problems, the computer presented the ideal solution to the subject. The participant then worked out how many moves the solutions required in their head, then touch the appropriate box on the screen to indicate the number of moves required.

***PAL***

The PAL test assessed visuo-spatial associative learning and contained both a delayed response procedure and a conditional learning task [47]. Participants would complete the eight stages in order. For each stage, boxes were displayed and opened in random order. The patterns shown in the boxes were then displayed in the screen center one by one. They were told to touch the box where the pattern was originally located with up to 10 trials at each stage. When they got all the correct locations, they would proceed to the next stage. If they could not complete a stage correctly, the test was terminated. Three indices were analyzed: (1) total errors; (2) total trials required to locate all the patterns correctly in all stages; (3) first trial memory scores; (4) mean errors and mean trials to success; and (5) stages completed on the first trial.

**Supplementary Results**

As shown in SFigure 4-5 (FDR-corrected significant results of t-test and F-test, respectively), the GFA of WM tracts of schizophrenia has more negative deviation and was more idiosyncratic than ASD or ADHD, with a remarkable trend that the longer duration of illness in schizophrenia was, the greater differences in deviation/idiosyncrasy relative to neurodevelopmental disorders were. That is, the absolute values of Cohen's d and F-ratio were larger in chronic schizophrenia vs. neurodevelopmental disorders pairs than those in the earlier stages (FES and subchronic schizophrenia) vs. neurodevelopmental pairs. Specifically, compared to patients with schizophrenia across the disease stages, people with ADHD, relative to people with ASD, showed greater extents (i.e., more tracts involved and larger effect sizes) of differences in deviance and idiosyncrasy. Among the neural tracts, the bilateral fornix (-1.04≤Cohen's d≤-0.44), frontostriatal tracts (-0.92≤Cohen's d≤-0.31), inferior fronto-occipital fasciculi (IFOF, -0.82≤Cohen's d≤-0.33), thalamic radiations (-0.64≤Cohen's d≤-0.33), corpus callosum-temporal lobe (-0.70≤Cohen's d≤-0.28), right perpendicular fasciculus (-0.84≤Cohen's d≤-0.31), and right superior longitudinal fasciculus-I (-0.72≤Cohen's d≤-0.28) showed prominent deviance patterns in schizophrenia, especially chronic schizophrenia (all Cohen's d≤-0.58 among these tracts), compared to neurodevelopmental disorders (SFigure 4A and SFigure 5A). Moreover, the thalamic radiations (1.48≤F-ratio≤2.87), frontostriatal tracts (1.40≤F-ratio≤2.67), and cingulum-hippocampal (1.61≤F-ratio≤2.66) showed the greatest differences in idiosyncrasy in schizophrenia (F-ratio $\geq$ 2.4) (SFigure 4B and SFigure 5B).

SFigure 7 and STable 8A show the overall similarity of deviance and idiosyncrasy of each tract between different stages of schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental disorders. Significant correlations were noted between GFA Z-score mean deviation of FES, and ASD ( *ρ* =0.49, *p* =.001) and ADHD ( *ρ* =0.42, *p* =.004), respectively. A significant correlation between GFA Z-score idiosyncrasy of FES was noted with ASD ( *ρ* =0.41, *p* =.005), but not with ADHD (SFigure 7B). Significant correlations were noted between GFA Z-score mean deviation of subchronic schizophrenia and ASD ( *ρ* =0.54, *p* &lt;.001) and ADHD ( *ρ* =0.52, *p* &lt;.001), respectively. A significant correlation between GFA Z-score idiosyncrasy of subchronic schizophrenia was noted with ASD ( *ρ* =0.32, *p* =.034), but marginally significant with ADHD ( *p* =.05). No correlation was noted between either deviation or idiosyncrasy in chronic schizophrenia and ASD or ADHD, respectively (SFigure 7B). The deviance in ASD had significantly higher correlations with that in FES than with that in chronic schizophrenia (FES-ASD 𝜌=0.49 &gt; chronic-ASD 𝜌=0.12; z=1.90, *p* =.028) (STable 8B).
