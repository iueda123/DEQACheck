1. MAJOR METHOD CATEGORIES WITH STUDY REFERENCES
================================================================================

The following categorization consolidates minor variations in method naming
(e.g., "GPR", "Gaussian Process Regression", "Gaussian process regression (GPR)")
into unified categories for documentation purposes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) - 31 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Non-parametric Bayesian method using kernel functions to model complex 
relationships between predictors and response variables. Highly flexible and
well-suited for capturing non-linear age trajectories.

Studies: Bayer2022, Chan2025B, Fang2024, Floris2021, FukamiGartner2023, 
Geng2025, Han2023, Han2024A, Han2024B, Holz2023, Huo2024, Ilioska2024, 
Janahi2022, Jia2024, Jia2025, Laidi2022, Liu2024, Looden2022, Ma2024, 
Parkes2021, Shan2022, Sun2023, Thukral2024, Wen2025, Wolfers2018, Wolfers2020, 
Wolfers2021, Xiao2024, Yu2024, Zabihi2019, Zabihi2020

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 Warped Bayesian Linear Regression (wBLR) - 16 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bayesian linear regression with likelihood warping (typically sinh-arcsinh or
SinArcsinh transformation) to handle non-normal distributions. Often implemented
with B-spline basis expansion for age effects.

Studies: Bhome2024, Cirstian2024, Echave2024, Fraza2023, Haukvik2025, 
Janssen2024, Kasper2024, Kim2023, Kim2024, Lamsma2024, Meijer2024, 
RehakBuckova2025, Rutherford2022, Rutherford2023, Segal2025, Verdi2024

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 GAMLSS (Generalized Additive Models for Location, Scale and Shape) - 15 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Flexible regression framework modeling all distribution parameters (location,
scale, shape) as functions of covariates. Can accommodate various distributions
(normal, gamma, Box-Cox t, SHASH, etc.) and smooth functions (splines, 
fractional polynomials).

Studies: Bedford2025, Bethlehem2021, Ebadi2024, GarciaSanMartin2025, Ge2024, 
Georgiadis2024, Huang2024, Lee2025, Leiberg2023, Little2025, Mao2025, 
Remiszewski2022, Tabbal2025, Wang2023, Young2024

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 Hierarchical Bayesian Regression (HBR) - 12 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bayesian regression with hierarchical/multi-level structure to model site or
scanner effects. Enables federated learning and improves generalizability across
datasets.

Studies: Berthet2025, Kia2022, Lawn2024, Loreto2024, Mansour2025, Segal2023, 
Sun2025, Verdi2023, VillalonReina2024, Worker2023, Wu2023, Wu2024

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5 Polynomial Regression - 11 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Linear regression with polynomial terms (linear, quadratic, cubic) or 
Multivariable Fractional Polynomial Regression (MFPR). Simple and interpretable
approach for modeling age trajectories.

Studies: Bethlehem2020, Coupe2022, Ge2024, Gordaliza2024, Haas2024, Jiang2024, 
Jing2023, Lin2023, Romascano2024, Yang2025, Zhang2023

Note: Includes MFPR (Multivariable Fractional Polynomial Regression) used in
CentileBrain framework (Haas2024, Yang2025, Ge2024, Gordaliza2024).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.6 Deep Learning Methods - 11 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Neural network-based approaches including Variational Autoencoders (VAE),
standard Autoencoders (AE), Adversarial Autoencoders (AAE), and hybrid 
approaches (VQ-VAE + GPT).

Breakdown:
- VAE/ConvVAE: Feng2024, Feng2025, Kumar2024, Kumar2025
- VQ-VAE + GPT: Mendes2024
- Autoencoder (AE): OliveiraSaraiva2023, Pinaya2019, Sampaio2025, Tong2024
- Adversarial Autoencoder (AAE): Pinaya2021, Vieira2025

Studies: Feng2024, Feng2025, Kumar2024, Kumar2025, Mendes2024, 
OliveiraSaraiva2023, Pinaya2019, Pinaya2021, Sampaio2025, Tong2024, Vieira2025

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.7 Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR) - 6 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Standard Bayesian linear regression without warping. Often uses B-spline basis
expansion for modeling non-linear age effects.

Studies: Echave2024, Floris2024, Giacomel2025, Kasper2024, Savage2024, Shao2024

Note: Echave2024 reports "BLR; also referred to as Warped BLR per Fraza 2021"
suggesting possible overlap with wBLR category.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.8 Quantile Regression - 4 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regression estimating conditional quantiles/percentiles (e.g., 5th, 50th, 95th)
rather than conditional means. Provides direct estimation of normative ranges.

Studies: DiBiase2022, Lin2024, Lv2021, Zheng2024

Note: Jing2023 and Lin2023 use "quantile polynomial regression" and are 
categorized under Polynomial Regression above.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.9 GAM/GAMM (Generalized Additive (Mixed) Models) - 4 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Generalized Additive Models with optional mixed/random effects. Uses smooth
functions (splines) for flexible non-linear relationships.

Studies: Chan2025A, Janssen2021, Leiberg2023, Little2024

Note: GAMLSS is a separate, more comprehensive framework and listed separately.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.10 Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LME/LMM) - 3 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Linear models with both fixed and random effects, typically modeling site or
subject-level variation.

Studies: CardenasDeLaParra2019, DeMeo2019, Jalbrzikowski2019

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.11 Similarity-Based Methods - 2 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Person-Based Similarity Index (PBSI) quantifying individual deviation from
normative patterns using correlation/similarity metrics rather than regression.

Studies: Baldwin2022, Joo2024

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.12 LOESS - 2 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Local polynomial regression fitting / locally estimated scatterplot smoothing.
Non-parametric approach fitting local regressions.

Studies: Jiang2024, Parkes2021

Note: Bethlehem2020 also used LOESS but is grouped under Polynomial methods.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.13 Other Specialized Methods - 16 studies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unique or highly specialized algorithms including:
- FUNCOIN (Kobbersmed2025): Functional connectivity normative modeling
- Nearest Neighbor (Leenings2024): Local density estimation
- Kernel Regression (Elad2021): Nadaraya-Watson
- Tolerance Intervals (Fang2025): Nonparametric edge-wise intervals
- Moving Average (Chien2022): Simple smoothing approach
- GLM (Ji2023, Zhang2022): General linear models
- And others with insufficient detail or proprietary methods

Studies: Chien2022, Elad2021, Fang2025, Giacomel2025, Hua2025, Italinna2023, 
Janssen2021, Ji2023, Kasper2024, Kim2023, Kobbersmed2025, Leenings2024, 
Martin2025, Tong2024, Zhang2022, Zhang2023

================================================================================
3. TRENDS AND OBSERVATIONS
================================================================================

3.1 Dominant Approaches:
- GPR is the most popular method (31 studies, 25.4%)
- Bayesian methods collectively dominate (GPR + HBR + wBLR + BLR = 65 instances)
- GAMLSS emerging as strong alternative for flexible distribution modeling

3.2 Method Evolution:
- Traditional: Polynomial regression, LME/LMM, LOESS
- Modern Bayesian: GPR, HBR, wBLR
- Advanced Distribution Modeling: GAMLSS, Quantile Regression
- Emerging: Deep learning (VAE, Autoencoders)

3.3 Specialized Applications:
- Multi-site data: HBR with hierarchical/federated structure
- Non-normal data: wBLR with likelihood warping, GAMLSS with flexible distributions
- Heterogeneity: PBSI for individual-level deviation patterns
- Functional connectivity: FUNCOIN for high-dimensional covariance modeling

3.4 Software Ecosystems:
- PCNtoolkit: GPR, BLR, wBLR, HBR implementations
- GAMLSS: R package for GAMLSS framework
- CentileBrain: MFPR implementation
- Custom/proprietary: Deep learning approaches, FUNCOIN, N³
